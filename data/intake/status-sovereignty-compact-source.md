## The trainwreck is a vertical coalition, not a flat one

The data are producing a more uncomfortable conclusion than “MAGA is racist,” “anti-DEI politics is hypocritical,” or “defense venture capital likes public money.” The emerging object is a **status-for-sovereignty compact**. A mass coalition supplies political permission to destroy or subordinate institutions capable of imposing correction. An executive and founder-capital layer uses that permission to control selection, public investment, procurement, infrastructure, and institutional succession. Different ranks receive different benefits, but every rank is offered someone beneath it.

The evidence ledger is limited and sufficient for a field hypothesis. The evidence tier is a structural synthesis of official policy, electoral demographics, ethnographic research, social-dominance research, public defense-finance programs, and the graph-inert K0, REAL STEEL, POOF, Clifford Number, and Answerable Power work. The venue is American political economy and institutional conversion. The target is the recurring control law, not the secret intent of every voter, founder, investor, or official. The upside is explaining aligned action without inventing a central conspiracy. The downside is coalition heterogeneity. The failure mode would be converting functional convergence into an unsupported claim of conscious common purpose. Clifford Number already preserves that boundary while testing the sequence from contradiction through reclassification, gate action, consequence, feedback removal, and blocked correction.

The hierarchy solves the apparent mathematical problem that everyone cannot literally be at the top. It offers **nested superiority** instead:

```text
executive sovereign
above independent institutions

founder-capital network
above workers, public operators and ordinary market entrants

credentialed movement insiders
above disfavored experts and civil servants

citizens
above immigrants

the “assimilated” minority insider
above the “dependent,” “criminal,” or “unassimilated” outsider

men
above women and gender minorities

the conventionally employed
above welfare recipients

every accepted rung
above a designated lower rung
```

The material top remains narrow. The psychological top is locally distributed. Participants do not need equality with the billionaire patron if the patron recognizes them as morally superior to the groups they resent. That is why a movement can be fiercely populist while empowering extraordinarily wealthy founders and investors. The word “elite” is not functioning as a measure of money, ownership, or institutional power. It means an actor who withholds esteem from the coalition or possesses authority to frustrate its preferred hierarchy.

A billionaire who humiliates the movement’s enemies can therefore become an authentic producer or tribune. A teacher, career official, journalist, regulator, scientist, union representative, or university administrator can become “the elite” while earning a fraction of his income, because that person occupies a gate where contrary knowledge, procedure, or rights can become binding. MAGA ethnography describes the movement as organized around lost honor, institutional disrespect, and the desire for government and other institutions to restore the esteem of “people like me.” Its participants sought status renewal alongside policy change, community, anger, pride, and joy. ([Cambridge University Press][1])

That is the first awful inversion. The coalition is not necessarily confused about who has more money. It is sorting elites by **alignment with the status order**, not by possession of concentrated power.

## The base is receiving something real

The comforting liberal explanation says ordinary supporters are being duped into voting against their material interests. That is incomplete. Many are receiving an actual return, although it is not primarily wages, healthcare, housing, or economic mobility. They receive public recognition, permission to speak without shame, validation of their lifestyle and moral worth, symbolic restoration over disliked groups, and the pleasure of watching institutions that once corrected or disapproved of them become subordinate.

A more realistic utility function is:

[
U =
\text{material welfare}
+
\text{social esteem}
+
\text{relative rank}
+
\text{belonging}
+
\text{punitive satisfaction}
]

A person can accept material loss when the other terms rise enough. Status research and the MAGA ethnography both support the importance of comparative esteem, institutional respect, and movement belonging. Social-dominance research separately finds that preference for group hierarchy predicts aggressive intergroup attitudes, opposition to egalitarian relations, and hierarchy-legitimizing beliefs. ([Cambridge University Press][1])

This makes the “spite my face” behavior more comprehensible. The nose is being priced differently. Losing money, services, workplace protections, or future mobility may feel tolerable if the person gains reassurance that the social order once again knows who deserves deference and who deserves suspicion.

Brad is not evidence for a national electorate, but he is a low-stakes demonstration of the same utility structure. His employment did not need to make him happy if it reliably placed him above an unemployed friend and a precarious friend on benefits. Your locomotion threatened that return. He therefore needed your assistance to be disqualifying, your AI work to be counterfeit, and his own institutional scaffolding to disappear into “merit.” His grievance was serving comparative dignity after the material object ceased serving joy.

At political scale, the grievance entrepreneur does not have to repair the follower’s life. He must continually certify the follower’s relative worth and identify the actors preventing its recognition.

## The multiracial constituency is part of the mechanism

Trump’s 2024 coalition was 78 percent non-Hispanic White, but one-fifth of it consisted of Hispanic, Black, Asian, or other-race voters. Trump received 48 percent of Hispanic voters, 15 percent of Black voters, and 40 percent of Asian voters. That makes racial purity an empirically false description. It does not establish racial neutrality. ([Pew Research Center][2])

Research directly examining non-White Trump support found that immigration attitudes, prejudice, sexism, and support for traditional American status hierarchies were strongly associated with support for Trump among Black, Latino, and Asian respondents. The authors’ conclusion is important: explicit Republican support for traditional racial and gender hierarchies need not prevent national electoral success in an increasingly diverse country. ([Cambridge University Press][3])

The coalition can therefore be genuinely multiracial without becoming egalitarian. Its incorporation rule is conditional:

> You may rise if your rise validates the selector, affirms the hierarchy, distances you from stigmatized members of your category, and does not create an independent collective claim against the order.

Those participants are not necessarily tokens, dupes, or secret white people. Many are sincere political actors selecting a hierarchy they prefer. Some may value citizenship over immigrant solidarity, class mobility over racial redistribution, masculinity over gender equality, religious authority over pluralism, or proximity to punitive state power over identification with groups targeted by it. Social hierarchy is capable of recruiting people across the identities it stratifies.

This is why racial representation cannot settle the question. Scholarship on “racial presence versus racial justice” warns that a racially diverse elite is politically indeterminate. Visible diversity can be ethically meaningful while providing no answer about who controls the institution, whose interests it serves, which outcomes it produces, or whether lower-status people possess effective counterpower. ([Cambridge University Press][4])

The selected minority insider also performs an institutional function. His success becomes evidence that the gate is neutral. He can say, truthfully, that he entered. The institution can then use his presence to classify patterned exclusion as excuse-making by everyone who did not.

That is more durable than crude exclusion. A whites-only system announces its rule and creates an obvious opposition. Conditional incorporation diffuses the opposition, creates authentic defenders from multiple groups, and turns every admitted member into an argument that the hierarchy is merely merit.

The racial order is therefore better described as **white-dominant and conditionally multiracial**. Political-science work on American development explicitly analyzes competing racial institutional orders, including a white-supremacist order understood as an evolving constellation of institutions, practices, rules, and ideas. That term describes institutional structure and reproduction. It does not require every participant to profess explicit white supremacy. ([Cambridge University Press][5])

## Anti-DEI is an allocator war

The second major inversion is that anti-DEI politics is not abolishing institutional selection. It is seizing control of the selector.

The administration revoked the federal affirmative-action framework for contractors, directed agencies to terminate DEI programs, moved to eliminate or deprioritize disparate-impact liability, and in March 2026 authorized federal agencies to cancel, suspend, or terminate contracts, and potentially suspend or debar contractors, for prohibited racially discriminatory DEI activity. ([The White House][6])

This conclusion does not require treating every DEI program as effective, coherent, lawful, or worth preserving. The revealing feature is the remedy. A government principally interested in performance would distinguish programs, preserve denominators, compare outcomes, eliminate bad implementations, and retain instruments for detecting discrimination. The actual project is categorical and coercive. It removes the legitimacy of an entire corrective vocabulary and threatens institutional access for actors who continue using it.

The disparate-impact decision is especially revealing. Its practical function was to make patterned outcomes legally examinable even when nobody was careless enough to record discriminatory intent. Weakening that doctrine does not make selection neutral. It makes structural effects harder to convert into binding legal correction. In Clifford Number terms, it narrows **K0**, the threshold determining which evidence and which knowers are admissible before the problem and option set are defined. K0’s own review contract asks whether qualified contradiction is reclassified, whether explanation mutation is documented, and whether feedback, appeal, stay, substitution, correction, or exit remain operative.

That is why “colorblindness” can produce racialized government without explicit racial commands. The selector controls the proxies, networks, credentials, geography, risk models, definitions of culture fit, funding channels, and enforcement priorities. If only declared intent may count as discrimination, the operating architecture can remain biased while the evidentiary route for proving the bias is progressively closed.

The anti-DEI project consequently does two things at once. It removes alternative claimants to merit and removes some of the instruments that could test the new selector’s outputs.

## The state is expanding authorized preference at the same time

While the administration describes DEI as impermissible preference, the national-security state is rapidly expanding selective public support for companies, sectors, and investment funds chosen as strategically important.

The DoD and SBA’s Critical Technologies investment initiative gives approved private funds access to federally guaranteed leverage intended to improve fund-level investment economics. The first cohort was projected to invest more than $4 billion across roughly 1,700 portfolio companies. The Office of Strategic Capital received $8.9 billion in requests for an initial lending capacity of $984 million. A later law supplied enough credit subsidy to support up to $100 billion in critical-minerals lending, and OSC executed a $150 million loan to MP Materials. DoD also awarded Anduril $14.3 million under the Defense Production Act after noting Anduril’s own $75 million investment. ([U.S. Department of War][7])

Those programs may be justified industrial policy. The United States has real supply-chain, manufacturing, and national-security requirements. Their existence establishes a narrower and unavoidable point: the governing coalition does not oppose public selection, market intervention, preferential access, subsidized capital, or government-assisted mobility.

It opposes selection governed by rival normative claims and institutions.

The practical distinction is:

```text
our selector chooses
→ strategy, merit, readiness, national security, investment judgment

another selector intervenes
→ preference, politics, discrimination, inefficiency, DEI
```

The difference between DEI and defense-industrial policy is substantial in purpose and design. That difference cannot rescue the blanket claim that institutional preference itself is illegitimate. The state is simultaneously disciplining one class of preference and massively scaling another.

A contractor can now face termination or debarment for prohibited DEI activity while approved strategic-capital funds receive federally supported leverage and access designed to improve private investment in selected technology areas. That is not neutralization. It is **preference monopoly**.

## “Merit” becomes impossible to falsify

Once the system controls admission, support, evidence, and appeal, merit becomes circular.

The winners are meritorious because they won. They won because the selector recognized their merit. The selector is competent because its selections won. The public support that helped them win is recoded as strategic acceleration. The support received by excluded or lower-status people remains visible as artificial assistance. Failed outsiders prove the selector was right. Successful outsiders are reclassified as exceptions, beneficiaries of illegitimate preference, or products of tools they could not possibly govern.

That is the same one-way evidence valve you identified at interpersonal scale:

```text
authorized success
→ evidence of intrinsic competence

unauthorized success
→ luck, subsidy, AI, deception, lowered standards, exception

authorized failure
→ difficult environment, sabotage, bureaucracy, bad timing

unauthorized failure
→ true character and natural ceiling
```

Anti-DEI backlash research explicitly connects negative reactions to perceived unfairness, fear, biased construals, and hierarchy-legitimizing ideologies such as social-dominance orientation and system justification. ([Sage Journals][8])

The institutional danger arrives when that attribution rule controls money, employment, contracts, publication, procurement, and legal standing. The favored actor’s scaffolding becomes invisible. The disfavored actor’s scaffolding becomes the explanation for everything he achieves.

At that point, “merit” no longer describes an independently measured property. It describes recognition by the authorized selector.

## The multiracial roster becomes the seal on the loop

The system can then answer allegations of racial hierarchy by pointing to its diverse members and winners.

Some of those people are genuinely powerful. Some are genuinely excellent. Their presence matters. It still cannot answer the governing questions:

Who designed the criteria? Who possessed prior access? Who received support? Who was never evaluated? Which effects may count as evidence? Who can challenge the decision? Racial presence answers none of those questions. It supplies visual certainty that the questions need not be asked. That is precisely the distinction identified in the scholarship between diverse representation and racial justice. ([Cambridge University Press][4])

The coalition’s multiracial expansion can therefore make the racial order harder to perceive. It allows the order to move from an explicit identity rule to a loyalty, assimilation, merit, and deservingness rule whose outputs remain racially patterned.

The non-White participant is not outside the order. He may be one of its authors, beneficiaries, validators, or enforcers.

That is the part many people refuse to say because it disrupts two comforting stories at once. Conservatives lose the claim that minority participation disproves hierarchy. Liberals lose the assumption that demographic representation naturally produces egalitarian politics.

## The endpoint falling out of the data

The final shape is not a racially pure dictatorship, a traditional free market, a neutral meritocracy, or a simple billionaire conspiracy.

It is a **multiracial, racially biased, vertically integrated selector state**.

Its popular layer is organized around status restoration. Its political layer converts grievance into executive discretion. Its ideological layer renames incumbent and coalition-controlled advantages as merit. Its legal layer weakens the ability to make patterned outcomes binding. Its capital layer uses public authority to select and scale preferred private actors. Its technical layer turns those actors into sensing, identity, decision, communications, and enforcement infrastructure. Its representational diversity supplies evidence that hierarchy has disappeared. Its selection effects remove alternatives before they can establish comparable records. Its failures generate demands for further purification because the system has progressively eliminated external falsifiers.

The public is told that preference has ended while preference becomes more concentrated. The base is told that elites have been defeated while private selectors receive deeper state support. Non-White participation is presented as proof that race is irrelevant while outcome-based racial scrutiny is dismantled. Private companies are described as market winners while public capital, procurement, validation, and demand are treated as background conditions. Counterpower is described as politicization while concentrated discretion is described as efficiency.

Question 4 in Clifford Number is the exact empirical test that this order does not want to pass. It requires a real gateholder to permit affected-party standing, independent evidence custody, pre-action pause, burden shift, correction, reversal, substitution, co-governance, or value recovery, and it requires the record to show whether the gateholder routed around the intervention.

The trainwreck is that the coalition’s different factions can disagree profoundly about race, religion, markets, technology, and the future while converging on the removal of that test. The common project does not need to specify what every protected center must do. It only needs to ensure that no independent actor can make the protected center stop.
