## Case object

**Public-interest question:** Which market-research, user-research, data, analysis, panel, interviewing, recruitment, and client-service functions are changed or displaced by synthetic populations, and what professional duties continue to govern the work?

**Selection unit:** one job function, workflow, headcount statement, budget claim, service contract, professional standard, methodology disclosure, client deliverable, redundancy, retraining programme, productivity claim, or quality-control responsibility.

**Selection universe:** all documented deployments and vendor claims concerning speed, volume, headcount, budget, automation, and professional roles across the frozen vendor cohort, plus matched conventional research organisations.

## Task packet

1. Define task-level substitution categories: assistance, acceleration, partial automation, role consolidation, full replacement, new work, and unresolved change.
2. Collect public productivity, headcount, budget, pricing, workflow, and organisational claims from vendors and customers.
3. Map which people remain accountable for sampling, questionnaire design, interpretation, quality assurance, client advice, consent, and sign-off.
4. Compare professional codes, disclosure duties, respondent treatment, synthetic-data labelling, and audit requirements across MRS, ESOMAR, AAPOR, and relevant regulators.
5. Search workforce reports, job postings, redundancies, retraining, union or employee statements, and procurement scopes without inferring causation from timing alone.
6. Construct matched workflow studies comparing synthetic, conventional, and hybrid research on cost, speed, quality, diversity, and error correction.

## Source families

- vendor and customer case studies
- professional association codes and guidance
- job postings and workforce disclosures
- procurement scopes and pricing materials
- employee, union, regulator, and audit records

## Predicate and inference boundary

**Allowed predicates:** `workflow_automated`, `research_volume_increased`, `headcount_claimed`, `human_signoff_required`, `professional_code_applies`, `role_retrained`, `redundancy_reported`.

**Forbidden inferences:** productivity proves job loss; job loss proves vendor caused it; automation eliminates professional duty; synthetic respondent is a human research subject by default; professional membership proves compliance.

## Acceptance

A task-level labour and accountability matrix with professional-standard mapping, matched workflows, and strict separation of productivity claims from verified employment effects.

Promotion requires human review and all repository gates. Until then, every observation remains `graph_effect: none`.

### Professional-standard map

**MRS**
- The April 2025 AI guidance applies to MRS members and Company Partners alongside the Code.
- It requires synthetic elements to be clearly identifiable, transparency/explainability, participant notice where AI processes personal data in unexpected ways, and continuing privacy/data-protection responsibility.
- Source: https://www.mrs.org.uk/standards/guidance-on-using-ai-and-related-technologies
- PDF: https://www.mrs.org.uk/pdf/AI%2BRelatedTechnologies_MRSGuidance_April2025.pdf

**ESOMAR**
- The 20 Questions are a buyer due-diligence framework covering provider competence, practical failures, explainability, human oversight, training-data quality, security, ownership, and data sovereignty.
- This is a disclosure/evaluation framework, not evidence that a supplier complies merely because it publishes answers.
- Source: https://community.esomar.org/uploads/public/library/documents/miscellaneous/20-questions-to-help-buyers-of-ai-based-services.pdf

**AAPOR**
- The revised June 2026 Code states AI-generated responses/digital twins/synthetic responses are not research participants; cases must be identified as AI-created; “poll,” “survey,” and related terms imply human respondents; AI generation method, AI tasks, and human oversight must be disclosed.
- AAPOR membership is not certification of professional competence.
- Source: https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/
- Supporting guidance: https://aapor.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Responsible-AI-Integration-In-Survey-Research.pdf

**ICO**
- Synthetic data can preserve patterns of source personal data and is not automatically anonymous; purpose, lawful basis, minimisation, and re-identification risk remain relevant.
- Source: https://ico.org.uk/media/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-sharing/privacy-enhancing-technologies-1-0.pdf

### Accountability baseline

The standards converge on duties that survive automation:

1. provenance and clear synthetic-data labelling;
2. distinction between human observation and modelled response;
3. explanation of AI's task and limitations;
4. lawful basis, privacy, and participant protections where personal data enters;
5. human responsibility for design, validation, interpretation, and disclosure;
6. no competence inference from professional membership or certification marks alone.

### Nearest boundary and next run

The next real artifact is a matched workflow packet—not a generic jobs forecast. Select one completed synthetic project and one comparable conventional/hybrid project, then capture task hours, people/roles, cost basis, elapsed time, validation failures, corrections, and sign-off. Separately search official workforce disclosures for dated headcount/redundancy events.

Acceptance remains **incomplete**: the professional-duty baseline and claim taxonomy are established, but verified labour effects and matched workflow economics are not.

## Second-pass receipt: MRS toolkit yields a task-level automation/accountability map

**Parent:** #30  
**Captured:** 2026-07-19  
**State:** `graph_effect: none`; professional guidance, not employment-effect evidence

### Baseline action

Inspected the current **MRS Data Analysis and AI Toolkit (2026)** and AAPOR's final **Revised June 2026 Code** at clause level:

- MRS toolkit: https://www.mrs.org.uk/pdf/MRS_AIAtoolkit_2026.pdf
- AAPOR Code: https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/

### Task-level workforce record

The MRS toolkit makes a more precise division than a generic “AI replaces researchers” claim:

| Task boundary | MRS-described change | Evidentiary classification |
|---|---|---|
| participant recruitment/data collection | synthetic participants can remove recruitment and compress collection from weeks/months | task automation and speed/cost claim |
| cleaning and cross-tabulation | described as mechanical work AI can automate | role acceleration / partial automation |
| classification and findings | practitioners must review, validate, and correct outputs | retained human QA responsibility |
| demographic fairness | models require continuing audit | new/expanded audit work |
| segmentation or risk modelling | leadership must define human intervention/override points | retained governance responsibility |
| interpretation, forecasting, storytelling | analyst role shifts toward strategic work | role redesign claim |
| ethical/strategic validation | identified as a high-value human contribution | retained accountability |

The toolkit's conclusion calls AI a co-pilot and says human oversight and critical thinking remain crucial. This is professional-body guidance and a task taxonomy; it is **not** a measured headcount outcome.

### Final AAPOR responsibility floor

The now-final June 2026 Code—not merely the earlier proposal—adds enforceable professional distinctions relevant to synthetic work:

- AI-generated responses are not human research participants and must be identified as AI-created;
- “poll” and “survey” imply human respondents and should not label AI-created data;
- researchers must use fit-for-purpose methods, avoid unwarranted confidence/generalisation, correct influential errors, and disclose methods sufficiently for independent verification;
- immediate disclosure includes instruments, population, sample-generation method, AI task, limitations, and human oversight;
- membership/adherence cannot be presented as certification of competence.

These duties remain attached to practitioners even when recruitment, collection, cleaning, coding, or reporting is automated.

### Labour-effect boundary

No dated redundancy, verified pre/post headcount, payroll, union statement, retraining cohort, or matched project cost ledger was recovered in this pass. The strongest supported predicates are therefore `workflow_automated`, `human_signoff_required`, and `professional_code_applies`; `redundancy_reported` remains unestablished.

### Nearest boundary

A matched workflow with task hours and named sign-offs before/after adoption, plus separate official workforce records. Productivity and professional-role redesign must remain distinct from employment displacement unless that second evidence stream appears.

No repository, PR, canonical, or graph state changed.
