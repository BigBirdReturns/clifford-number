## Candidate seams

### F-01: Survey-panel and data-broker supply chain

Map the panel providers, audience measurement firms, first-party data holders, brokers, licensors, survey archives, and public datasets that supply persona construction, calibration, or validation. Promotion requires a named vendor-to-source disclosure or contract, not an inference from demographic similarity.

### F-02: Synthetic persona likeness, identity, and intellectual-property rights

Test whether personas reproduce distinctive real-person, creator, respondent, employee, celebrity, patient, or community attributes; identify consent, publicity, copyright, database, trade-secret, and contractual claims. Promotion requires a named product feature, dispute, licence, or policy.

### F-03: Contestability and the right to challenge a modelled public

Identify who may inspect, correct, appeal, or override a synthetic representation used in a consequential decision, and whether affected groups can contest being represented by it. Promotion requires a named deployment and decision process.

### F-04: Public consultation and regulatory-impact substitution

Search government consultations, regulatory impact assessments, policy design, legislative research, public communications, and service design for synthetic-population use. Separate internal scenario testing from replacing statutory or voluntary consultation. Promotion requires an official procurement, policy document, disclosure, or meeting record.

### F-05: Crisis, emergency, and national-resilience simulation

Map use in pandemics, disaster response, civil contingencies, public-order planning, evacuation, supply chains, energy shocks, and national resilience. Promotion requires a named programme, contract, exercise, grant, or official capability statement.

### F-06: Healthcare, patient, and public-health populations

Map synthetic patients, treatment preferences, adherence, health communication, clinical-trial recruitment, public-health messaging, and service planning. Preserve medical-device, research-ethics, patient-consent, and health-data boundaries. Promotion requires a named health deployment or regulated product claim.

### F-07: Employment, hiring, and synthetic workforces

Test use in hiring, employee sentiment, organisational design, workforce planning, labour negotiations, training, and product testing through simulated employees. Promotion requires a named employer, vendor case, procurement, or employment-policy disclosure.

### F-08: Credit, insurance, pricing, and financial-consumer simulation

Search banks, insurers, fintechs, asset managers, credit providers, and regulators for synthetic consumer populations used in product design, pricing, risk, collections, conduct, or stress testing. Promotion requires a named regulated deployment or filing.

### F-09: Advertising currencies and measurement governance

Trace PAMCo, JICs, audience currencies, clean rooms, customer-data platforms, identity systems, attribution tools, and synthetic personas into advertiser-facing products. Promotion requires a documented data or product integration, not shared industry membership.

### F-10: Standards, certification, and audit markets

Map MRS, ESOMAR, AAPOR, ISO, NIST, IEEE, regulator guidance, private assurance, model audits, and emerging synthetic-data certifications. Ask who defines conformity, who pays, what is tested, and whether certification scopes the deployed system. Promotion requires a standard, audit scheme, certification, or enforcement record.

### F-11: Patents, trade secrets, and publicly funded intellectual property

Search patent families, grants, university spin-outs, public research funding, software repositories, and founder publications for technical lineage. Do not infer trade-secret transfer from thematic similarity. Promotion requires a patent, grant, licence, assignment, or public repository.

### F-12: Liability, indemnification, and insurance

Determine who bears loss when a synthetic population produces misleading, discriminatory, unlawful, or commercially harmful output: vendor, customer, model provider, data supplier, researcher, or insurer. Promotion requires public contract terms, litigation, insurance language, regulator action, or a disclosed incident.

### F-13: Incident reporting and acceptable-use enforcement

Build a corpus of prohibited-use policies, enforcement actions, customer suspensions, safety incidents, disclosed failures, corrections, and model withdrawals. A policy statement is evidence of a stated rule, not enforcement. Promotion requires a public incident, enforcement record, or sufficiently complete policy denominator.

### F-14: Resellers, consultancies, and white-label deployment

Map deployments hidden behind research agencies, consultancies, systems integrators, resellers, advertising platforms, and white-label products. Promotion requires a receipted prime/subcontractor, reseller, integration, or customer record.

### F-15: Acquisition, insolvency, and data/model custody

Track what happens to customer data, persona libraries, validation corpora, prompts, models, contracts, and audit duties when vendors are acquired, fail, restructure, or change control. Promotion requires a filed capital event and publicly addressable custody terms or customer notice.

### F-16: Public-record retention, FOI, and audit trails

Ask whether synthetic-population prompts, persona definitions, outputs, decision logs, model versions, and vendor communications become public records when governments use them, and whether retention permits later review. Promotion requires a named public deployment, records schedule, FOI response, audit report, or litigation hold.

### F-17: Group privacy and vulnerable-population representation

Test harms that remain after individual anonymisation, including small communities, protected classes, children, migrants, patients, political minorities, and geographically concentrated groups. Promotion requires a named dataset, deployment, regulator analysis, or documented failure.

### F-18: Election-law classification of synthetic public opinion

Determine when synthetic polling, message testing, campaign research, coordinated expenditure, in-kind services, political advertising, or voter modelling triggers disclosure, polling, campaign-finance, data-protection, or election-integrity rules. Promotion requires a jurisdiction-specific legal source and a named capability or deployment.

### F-19: Upstream model drift and warranty continuity

Test whether warranties, accuracy claims, audits, and customer representations survive foundation-model, retrieval, prompt, persona, weighting, or post-processing changes. Promotion requires a versioned change and a claim whose validation status can be compared before and after.

### F-20: Synthetic-population market concentration and dependency

Measure concentration across vendors, foundation models, clouds, data suppliers, analyst categories, investors, and large customers. Distinguish ordinary common infrastructure from control or coordination. Promotion requires a frozen vendor denominator and resolved legal entities.

## Promotion rubric

A candidate is promoted only when all of the following are present:

1. a public-interest question that survives removal of Electric Twin and any named political actor;
2. a neutral selection unit and a bounded, source-complete, rule-based, or matched-comparator universe;
3. at least one addressable positive anchor and one plausible negative or ordinary control;
4. allowed predicates and forbidden inferences specific enough to prevent generic association;
5. privacy, legal-status, and protected-person handling appropriate to the seam;
6. a reproducible source path and measurable coverage state;
7. an acceptance test that can return positive, negative, inconclusive, or inadmissible without narrative pressure.

Until promotion, every item is `graph_effect: none`, and absence from the queue or corpus proves nothing.

### The new structural artifact

Omnicom completed its acquisition of Interpublic on 26 November 2025, then launched the combined new Omni on 7 January 2026. Omni combines Acxiom RealID, Flywheel transaction data, media buying, activation, autonomous agents and measurement. Annalect is unusually explicit that the platform is data-agnostic and can **switch data providers in and out**.

That provider-switching statement is the clearest current evidence for the market-shape hypothesis: a point synthetic-population vendor can become an upstream module while the holding-company platform retains client context, identity mapping, workflow authority, activation, spend, measurement and the feedback loop.

The Netflix path is operationally important but must not be misclassified. Omnicom says the offering is already live in the US and joins Acxiom audience intelligence, Omni, generative creative, “digital twin technology” and closed-loop measurement. The described twin appears to be a creative/content representation used to fit brand assets into programming, not a simulated public or synthetic respondent population.

### Positive inference, bounded

The evidence now supports this **investigative market-shape inference**:

> The scarce asset may not be the ability to generate synthetic personas. It may be control of the admission-and-action plane that decides which representation enters client workflow, which data enriches it, where money is activated, how outcomes are measured, and when the upstream engine is replaced.

This is not yet a concentration statistic, market-share finding, coordination claim or graph fact. It identifies four controller candidates under a common functional screen.
