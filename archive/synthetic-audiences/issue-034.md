## Case object

**Public-interest question:** Which population-response, testing, forecasting, dashboard, behavioural, logistics, and executive-decision capabilities developed or deployed during the pandemic later became commercial products, consultancies, investment theses, or reusable government services?

**Selection unit:** one named pandemic programme, tool, dataset, contractor deliverable, public official, product launch, company formation, acquisition, patent, funding round, or later customer deployment.

**Selection universe:** all capabilities and suppliers enumerated from a frozen set of official UK pandemic programme, inquiry, NAO, spending, and contract sources, followed forward without selecting only famous founders.

The target is an institutional or capability pattern, not the private character of any person. The selection rule must admit an analogous actor or institution under the same records regardless of party, ideology, nationality, affiliation, investor identity, or anticipated news value. Missing records, failed queries, unresolved identities, ordinary career movement, and evidence against the working hypothesis remain first-class output.

## Task packet

1. Enumerate the capability and supplier denominator from official pandemic records before searching later companies or products.
2. Classify each capability by function, data inputs, decision authority, public/private ownership, and operational venue.
3. Trace subsequent company formations, product launches, acquisitions, patents, grants, and public or commercial deployments using identity-grade evidence.
4. Document data-retention, intellectual-property, confidentiality, and business-appointment controls where public.
5. Compare capabilities that commercialised with those that remained public, were retired, or produced no visible successor.
6. Preserve the ordinary explanation that transferable expertise naturally produces later products unless stronger records establish a specific asset or programme transfer.

## Source families

- UK Covid-19 Inquiry
- NAO pandemic reports
- Contracts Finder and departmental spending
- Companies House and patent records
- company product and funding announcements

## Predicate and inference boundary

**Allowed predicates:** `deployed_during_pandemic`, `supplier_to_programme`, `later_commercialised_as`, `founded_after_role`, `acquired_by`, `received_public_contract`.

**Forbidden inferences:** later similarity proves technology transfer; pandemic role proves access to reusable personal data; company success proves state sponsorship; contractor status proves policy control; no public successor means no commercialisation.

Co-presence is never coordination. A broad department, company, programme family, or professional network is not a Clifford hop. Any eventual surface proposal must name the bounded object, participants, roles, dates, receipts, evidence class, temporal overlap, density treatment, and copy-ready caveat.

## Acceptance

A source-complete capability denominator and forward lineage matrix with verified positives, explicit nulls, identity holds, and no causal claim beyond documented transfer or later reuse.

Promotion requires a human-reviewed candidate packet and the repository's selection, consumption, privacy, temporal, density, receipt, and release gates. Until then, all observations remain research intake with `graph_effect: none`.

## First bounded evidence receipt: one pandemic-capability successor path

**Parent:** #30  
**Captured:** 2026-07-19  
**Status:** research intake only; `graph_effect: none`

### Baseline action

Followed one capability from its official pandemic operating record through the later procurement and award:

- NHS COVID-19 Data Store roles: https://www.england.nhs.uk/nhs-covid-19-data-store/
- FDP procurement prospectus (project C104091; published 10 Jan 2023): https://www.england.nhs.uk/aac/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/PRN00389-nhs-federated-data-platform-and-associated-services.pdf
- Award/update, 7 Dec 2023: https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/federated-data-platform-update/
- FDP privacy notice: https://www.england.nhs.uk/digitaltechnology/nhs-federated-data-platform/security-privacy/nhs-fdp-privacy-notice/

### Observed artifact

**Pandemic baseline.** NHS England identifies the COVID-19 Data Store as an Azure-hosted environment, with Palantir Foundry supporting the platform and Faculty, McKinsey and Deloitte working under contract on analytical capability. It explicitly identifies NHS England as data controller and describes pseudonymised/aggregate/anonymous access levels.

**Forward lineage.** The 2023 prospectus expressly says the competitive procurement would **replace** the Palantir-provided COVID-19 Datastore and required a safe transition from it. It also states that:

- the datastore was created during Covid for public health, healthcare administration and pandemic response;
- it was later used for NHS recovery and some direct patient care;
- NHS England had already piloted modules/use cases on it;
- the existing call-off ran through June 2023;
- about 30 trusts used the existing solution.

**Later award.** NHS England records an FDP-AS award on 22 Nov 2023 to a Palantir-led consortium with Accenture, PwC, Carnall Farrar and NECS, and says products would transition from the National Data Platform to FDP.

### Classification and refusal

This is a documented **reusable-government-service / competitively procured successor** row. It is not yet evidence that a pandemic-developed asset was privately appropriated or commercialised as a vendor-owned product. Same lead supplier does not by itself prove IP transfer, data reuse for the supplier's purposes, or a no-bid continuation.

The current FDP privacy notice names Palantir and IQVIA as processors and states they may process personal data only for providing FDP services on written instructions, not for their own purposes. That is a control receipt, not proof of perfect compliance.

### Nearest remaining boundary

The official pandemic programme/supplier denominator is not frozen. This row should be retained alongside nulls and retired capabilities, then decomposed into platform, modules, code, data, contracts and legal counterparties. Exact product-by-product transition receipts remain unresolved.

No repository, canonical, or graph state changed.
