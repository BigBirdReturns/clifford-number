## Case object

**Public-interest question:** What public-sector, campaign, health, defence, and enterprise capabilities, personnel, products, contracts, and governance obligations moved when Accenture acquired Faculty?

**Selection unit:** one filed acquisition event, named product, employee cohort, director or executive transition, disclosed contract, public-sector framework, customer case, intellectual-property statement, or post-acquisition service.

**Selection universe:** all Faculty legal entities, named products, directors, publicly disclosed customers and contracts, and Accenture units explicitly connected to the transaction, frozen before evaluating political significance.

The target is an institutional or capability pattern, not the private character of any person. The selection rule must admit an analogous actor or institution under the same records regardless of party, ideology, nationality, affiliation, investor identity, or anticipated news value. Missing records, failed queries, unresolved identities, ordinary career movement, and evidence against the working hypothesis remain first-class output.

## Task packet

1. Freeze the corporate transaction chronology, legal entities, consideration disclosures if public, director changes, ownership changes, and completion date.
2. Inventory Faculty products, documented government and private deployments, contracts, frameworks, research partnerships, and regulated-sector work before acquisition.
3. Track named personnel and product placement after acquisition, including Marc Warner's role and any retained brand, platform, or operating autonomy.
4. Test whether public-sector contracts were novated, re-tendered, subcontracted, expired, or continued through frameworks.
5. Compare with other Accenture acquisitions of specialist AI firms using the same capability-transfer predicates.
6. Separate acquisition-scale facts from claims about political influence, market power, or government dependence.

## Source families

- Accenture transaction announcements and filings

- UK procurement portals and departmental spending

- CMA or other competition records if applicable

## Predicate and inference boundary

**Allowed predicates:** `acquired_by`, `transaction_completed_on`, `executive_transitioned_to`, `product_transferred_to`, `contract_novated`, `framework_supplier`, `customer_case_published`.

**Forbidden inferences:** acquisition transfers every relationship; government customer proves influence; founder history defines acquirer conduct; employment continuity proves policy continuity; reported transaction value proves consideration paid.

Co-presence is never coordination. A broad department, company, programme family, or professional network is not a Clifford hop. Any eventual surface proposal must name the bounded object, participants, roles, dates, receipts, evidence class, temporal overlap, density treatment, and copy-ready caveat.

## Acceptance

A transaction-centred capability ledger showing what demonstrably moved, what remained separate, what was merely marketed as continuity, and which public contracts or programmes changed legal counterparty.

Promotion requires a human-reviewed candidate packet and the repository's selection, consumption, privacy, temporal, density, receipt, and release gates. Until then, all observations remain research intake with `graph_effect: none`.

### What is **not** demonstrated

The opened sources do not establish novation, re-tendering, expiry or continuation of any particular UK public contract; transfer of every IP asset; transfer of customer relationships; or operating autonomy beyond the retained active company and CEO title. Acquisition cannot be used as a generic edge for those objects.
