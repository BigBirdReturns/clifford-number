## Public-interest question

When professional research bodies address AI-generated, synthetic, silicon, or digital-twin responses, what exactly must be disclosed, validated, labelled, or governed; to whom do those duties apply; and what enforcement or assurance mechanism exists?

This question survives removal of Electric Twin and every named vendor or political actor.

## Neutral selection unit

One versioned, publicly accessible official code, binding rule, guidance document, buyer checklist, interpretation, disciplinary procedure, upheld complaint, audit/certification scheme, or revision record published by one body in the frozen denominator.

## Frozen denominator and symmetric rule

**Bodies:** Market Research Society (MRS), ESOMAR/ICC, and American Association for Public Opinion Research (AAPOR).

**Document universe:** every current public official document linked from each body's standards/code surface as of **2026-07-19** that:

1. expressly contains one or more of: `artificial intelligence`, `AI`, `synthetic data`, `synthetic response`, `silicon response/sample`, `digital twin`, or `generated response`; or
2. defines the applicability, complaint, disciplinary, assurance, audit, or certification mechanism for a document selected by rule 1.

Apply the same term and link-following rule to all three bodies. Freeze URL, title, issuing body, publication/revision date, retrieved date, and file hash when downloadable. Broken, paywalled, superseded, and absent documents remain coverage states; they are not silently dropped.

## Positive anchors

- MRS, **Guidance on Using AI and Related Technologies** (April 2025), explicitly applies to MRS members and Company Partners and includes synthetic-data requirements:  
  https://www.mrs.org.uk/standards/guidance-on-using-ai-and-related-technologies
- ESOMAR, **20 Questions to Help Buyers of AI-Based Services**, includes supplementary questions for generative AI and synthetic data, including real-world validation and labelling:  
  https://community.esomar.org/uploads/public/library/documents/miscellaneous/20-questions-to-help-buyers-of-ai-based-services.pdf
- ICC/ESOMAR, **International Code on Market, Opinion and Social Research and Data Analytics** (2025), expressly addresses AI and synthetic data and defines compliance/implementation responsibilities:  
  https://standards.esomar.org/assets/documents/icc-esomar-code-2025.pdf
- AAPOR, **Code of Professional Ethics and Practices** (revised June 2026), distinguishes AI-generated responses from participants and requires generated cases to be identified:  
  https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/

## Negative and ordinary controls

For each body, capture:

- the immediately preceding official code/guidance version, if public, to test whether synthetic-specific text is new, unchanged, or absent;
- the body's general code/standards root and one in-scope general research rule with no synthetic-specific term, if present;
- a null row when no public enforcement, audit, certification, or upheld-complaint record matches the selected document.

Historical absence is only a version comparison. Non-membership is not evidence of noncompliance.

### Clause-level observations

**MRS.** Its official standards page dates the current guidance to April 2025, says it applies to all MRS members and Company Partners, and says it must be read with the MRS Code. This supports `applies_to_member`, `applies_to_company_partner`, and `publishes_instrument`; it does not by itself support certification or recorded enforcement.

**ESOMAR checklist.** In the synthetic-data supplement to question 7, buyers are directed to ask what validation compares synthetic outputs with primary research or real-world results, what recency criteria apply, and how natural-person-derived data is distinguished from synthetic data. The document is expressly a checklist/guidance for commissioning AI services, so these are buyer questions—not proven vendor duties or audit findings.

**ICC/ESOMAR Code.** The 2025 code describes itself as self-regulation, says it is mandatory for ESOMAR members and adopting bodies, covers AI including synthetic data, and requires researchers to ensure clients/subcontractors agree to applicable requirements. Article 12 requires cooperation with disciplinary investigation. This supports a documented compliance mechanism, not evidence that any synthetic-population vendor has been investigated or sanctioned.

**AAPOR.** The revised June 2026 code makes the sharpest terminology rule recovered so far: AI-generated responses are not research participants; AI-created cases must be identified; and terms such as poll/survey imply human respondents and should not describe AI-created data. Its disclosure section separately requires describing how AI-generated responses were made, the AI task, and human oversight/validation.

| Body | Prior control | Current instrument | What changed, at the level the sources support |
|---|---|---|---|
| MRS | [November 2023 AI guidance](https://www.mrs.org.uk/pdf/MRS_AI_Guidance.pdf), version V1123 | [April 2025 AI and Related Technologies guidance](https://www.mrs.org.uk/pdf/AI%2BRelatedTechnologies_MRSGuidance_April2025.pdf) | The 2023 guidance already defined synthetic data and imposed general AI transparency, auditability, human-oversight and validity duties. The 2025 edition adds a discrete **Data Creation and Use** section: participant consent for personal data used to make synthetic models; client consent for synthetic-data techniques; records of generation, combination, bias/error mitigation, refresh and training sources; and identification of each synthetic element when mixed with non-synthetic data. |
| ICC/ESOMAR | [official ESOMAR catalogue record for the 7 Dec 2016 Code](https://ana.esomar.org/documents/icc-esomar-international-code) | [ICC/ESOMAR Code updated July 2025](https://standards.esomar.org/assets/documents/icc-esomar-code-2025.pdf) | The current Code calls 2016 the immediately preceding update and describes AI, including synthetic data, as part of the revision context; it emphasizes accountability, transparency and human oversight. The 2016 catalogue record is reached, but byte-stable prior English PDF custody is still incomplete: the long-lived English-PDF URL now resolves to revised text. |
| AAPOR | April 2021 Code, retained in AAPOR's [2021 disclosure-standard page](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/disclosure-standards/) | [Code revised June 2026](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/) | AAPOR's official [change summary](https://aapor.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Summary-of-changes-for-membership-vote.pdf) and [marked comparison](https://aapor.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Code-changes-with-member-suggested-edits-flagged.pdf) identify the human/AI distinction as new language: AI-generated cases are not participants, must be identified, should not be called polls/surveys when AI-created, and now carry AI-task, oversight, validation and sample-generation disclosures. |

### Instrument type and applicability

**MRS:** this remains **guidance**, but it is not merely optional advice. The document states that “must” provisions are mandatory applications of the MRS Code for MRS Members and Company Partners; “should” denotes recommended practice. Its [complaint process](https://www.mrs.org.uk/standards/complaint-handling) investigates those covered parties through the MRS Standards Board, with contested findings able to proceed to the Disciplinary Authority.

**ICC/ESOMAR:** the 2025 instrument is a **self-regulatory Code**, mandatory for ESOMAR members and adopting associations/bodies. Its [complaints surface](https://standards.esomar.org/resources/complaints-and-disciplinary-process) says all members must cooperate; non-member cases are discretionary. Sanctions can range from private warnings to expulsion and publication.

**AAPOR:** the 2026 instrument is a professional **Code**, subscribed to by members and expressed as expected principles for the wider profession. The [violation schedule](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/schedule-of-procedures-for-code-violatiosn/) allows any person to complain about an individual, firm or organization, including a non-member. Complaint materials are confidential; public action is discretionary. AAPOR membership and Code adherence are not certification of professional competence.

### Enforcement and assurance surfaces

- **MRS:** the live upheld-complaints page currently exposes one non-synthetic MRSB decision and no Disciplinary Authority decision. It says public statements remain online for only three months and older records require a request to MRS. Therefore the public page cannot support an all-time zero.
- **ESOMAR:** [2017–2018 complaints reporting](https://community.esomar.org/uploads/public/knowledge-and-standards/documents/ESOMAR-Complaints-Report-2017-2018.pdf) demonstrates that the mechanism has produced counts, escalations and public notifications, but it predates the 2025 synthetic-data revision. Current procedures permit private warnings and allow some publication decisions to remain non-public.
- **AAPOR:** the complaint mechanism can produce public or private censure. Separately, the [Transparency Initiative](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/transparency-initiative/) reviews disclosure examples and rechecks members every two years, but expressly does **not** judge methodological quality or rigor. TI status is therefore general disclosure assurance, not synthetic-response validation or certification.

- ESOMAR prior version identity reached: **yes; prior English bytes not yet frozen**

### Discovery

The standards did not all “add synthetic research rules” at once. MRS moved from broad AI duties to transaction-level consent, record and labelling duties; AAPOR added a categorical human-versus-generated boundary plus disclosure duties; ICC/ESOMAR revised a global self-regulatory Code around AI-era accountability. Enforcement visibility is asymmetric enough that a clean public-web search cannot support a comparative enforcement-rate claim.
