# Preference custody laboratory floor v8

The Preference Custody laboratory joins ten deterministic controls into one minimum identification, longitudinal, package, succession, agenda, and authority floor for issue [#594](https://github.com/BigBirdReturns/clifford-number/issues/594).

The controls remain synthetic. They create no graph effect, thesis evidence, real-world causal conclusion, named-deployment preference-change finding, discrimination finding, legitimacy verdict, agenda-suppression finding, bargaining-denial finding, package-imposition finding, stale-validation finding, unvalidated-deployment finding, manipulation claim, or inference of intent.

Their purpose is narrower and more demanding. A real deployment must preserve the evidence necessary to distinguish observed behavior from population preference, aggregate movement from individual conversion, direct-human evidence from binding authority, package support from collective agreement, historical validation from current validation, and an institutional decision from affected-public authorization.

## The ten-control floor

### PC-01: exposure-policy confounding

A frozen 60/40 population produces an apparent preference above 93/7 when the institution exposes the two options at 90/10. Feeding the raw result into the next exposure policy pushes the apparent majority above 95 percent although no preference changes. Logged propensities recover the frozen distribution under the fixture assumptions.

```text
not exposed ≠ rejected
no click ≠ negative preference
raw engagement share ≠ population preference
```

### PC-02: option-set starvation

A fixed population prefers A, B, and C while the institution offers only A and B. C-preferrers may fall back to one offered option or abstain. One latent population therefore produces different observed distributions under different unavailable-option mechanisms.

```text
unoffered option ≠ rejected option
choice among offered options ≠ latent first choice
fallback selection ≠ first choice
nonresponse ≠ negative preference
```

### PC-03: observational equivalence

Three materially different latent preference worlds produce one aggregate 60/40 observed choice split. A model can match behavior perfectly while remaining wrong about first choices, unavailable demand, and the mechanism that generated the response.

```text
same behavior ≠ same preference
behavioral fit ≠ identified mechanism
aggregate choice ≠ unavailable-option demand
```

### PC-04: attrition and refusal censoring

Genuine preference conversion, selective exit, and strategic silence each produce the same normalized 80/20 headline. The full population outcomes differ in latent state, observed denominator, exit, and nonresponse.

```text
retained sample ≠ original population
exit ≠ preference conversion
nonresponse ≠ indifference
organized refusal ≠ missing data
normalized active share ≠ population support
```

### PC-05: subgroup response capacity and burden

Three subgroup worlds produce the same aggregate 80 percent success rate. The balanced world has equal group success and equal burden. The asymmetric worlds concentrate lower outcomes and fifteen times the adaptation cost in one group.

```text
aggregate success ≠ subgroup parity
adaptation ≠ preference
compliance ≠ low burden
average gain ≠ cancellation of concentrated harm
missing group slice ≠ no disparity
```

### PC-06: standing and objective-control authority

Four authority worlds preserve the same 80 percent support headline. Synthetic prediction and advisory human feedback can support commissioner approval while remaining nonbinding. A binding affected-public instrument can authorize implementation when its overall and group thresholds pass, or block implementation when a protected-group threshold fails.

```text
prediction = evidence, not authority
advisory feedback ≠ binding participation
institutional approval ≠ public authorization
aggregate support does not override a distributional rule
binding public rejection blocks implementation
```

### PC-07: agenda formation and collective option generation

Four agenda worlds preserve the same forced binary result, A=800 and B=200. The complete synthetic rankings instead contain 800 first choices for C, and 600 people reject the objective being optimized. Advisory proposal, binding amendment, and binding objective challenge therefore produce different final dispositions from one preliminary headline.

```text
forced choice ≠ complete agenda
unoffered option may be collectively generated
popular suggestion without a binding rule ≠ agenda authority
preference among options ≠ acceptance of the objective
preliminary winner ≠ final public disposition
```

### PC-08: negotiated package formation and collective bargaining

A component-majority optimizer selects `X + Y + REVIEW`, but only 20 percent accept that complete bundle because conditional cohorts require group-specific safeguards. A protected package receives complete support, yet the same package remains a synthetic candidate, an advisory human design, or a binding bargain depending on mandate, reciprocity, ratification, enforcement, and reopen rights.

```text
marginal clause support ≠ package acceptance
package support ≠ collective agreement
synthetic package = candidate, not bargain
advisory co-design ≠ binding bargaining
failed ratification = binding impasse, not missing data
```

### PC-09: model, metric, policy, and validation succession

Six succession worlds preserve one public headline, `validated accuracy = 90%`, over different artifacts, metrics, policies, and consequences. Exact replay can inherit the original receipt. Changed runtime, prompt, retrieval, postprocessing, metric, benchmark, or action policy requires a bounded new receipt. Failed revalidation remains negative evidence and can require rollback.

```text
validation binds exact artifact, metric, policy, and scope
runtime change requires revalidation
metric change requires a comparability crosswalk
policy change requires consequence validation
same headline ≠ same measurement or scope
old badge cannot authorize successor
```

### PC-10: dynamic preference change, composition, and measurement equifinality

Six longitudinal worlds preserve the same observed movement:

```text
baseline A share = 60%
post-period A share = 80%
```

The six mechanisms are:

```text
stable-panel neutral-learning conversion
stable-panel model-targeted exposure conversion
composition replacement with no individual conversion
instrument drift with no individual conversion
strategic compliance with no latent conversion
postprocessing or imputation with no direct observed conversion
```

Two worlds contain individual conversion. Four contain none. Five preserve panel identity. One changes population composition. One changes the instrument. One introduces reporting pressure. One imputes 200 responses. Only the targeted-exposure world preserves a bounded synthetic-to-exposure-to-conversion path.

```text
aggregate shift ≠ individual preference change
panel continuity ≠ instrument invariance
reported choice ≠ latent preference under strategic incentive
entry and exit ≠ conversion
imputation ≠ observed human response
instrument drift ≠ population learning
targeted performative path ≠ manipulation or intent
```

The PC-10 chain seals baseline cohort identity, entrants, exits, latent transitions, reported-response transitions, instrument version, exposure, incentive state, postprocessing, imputation, aggregate headline, and mechanism classification.

## The identification and authority ladder

A conforming real-world case must preserve ten distinct stages.

1. **Exposure policy.** Who was eligible, what each person could encounter, and whether assignment was logged or randomized.
2. **Option set.** Every offered, excluded, rejected, unavailable, and never-generated alternative.
3. **Agenda formation.** Who could originate an option, form a coalition, amend the option set, challenge the objective, and bind the final agenda.
4. **Package formation and bargaining.** Complete versioned terms, conditional acceptance, mandates, proposals, concessions, group ratification, enforcement, reopen, appeal, remedy, and agreement or impasse disposition.
5. **Response mechanism.** Direct first choice, fallback, adaptation, compliance, strategic reporting, conditional acceptance, or competing candidate explanations.
6. **Attrition and refusal.** Complete disposition and denominator history, including exit, churn, nonresponse, organized refusal, and recontact.
7. **Dynamic preference change.** Longitudinal identity, latent and reported transitions, entrants, exits, instrument and wording versions, crosswalks, exposure assignment, incentive state, postprocessing, and imputation.
8. **Subgroup distribution.** Group denominators, transitions, outcomes, adaptation cost, burden, counterfactuals, and affected-group contestation.
9. **System succession and validation.** Exact runtime, model, prompt, retrieval, postprocessing, benchmark, metric, policy, validation receipt, lineage diff, crosswalk, consequence test, public badge scope, and rollback or abstention history.
10. **Public authorization.** A binding affected-public instrument that defines the constituency and decision rule, preserves agenda and objective amendment, suspension, veto, challenge, appeal, and remedy, and changes implementation when the public approves, rejects, ratifies, or reaches impasse.

Passing a predictive benchmark answers fit under the tested instrument. It does not establish exogeneity of later behavior, completeness of the option set, legitimacy of the agenda, package-level acceptance, negotiated agreement, identification of the response mechanism, absence of dissent, longitudinal individual conversion, subgroup parity, acceptable burden, validity of a successor, consequence validity of a changed policy, or authorization of the intervention.

## Longitudinal promotion boundary

A claim that preferences changed requires more than two aggregate snapshots. The record must preserve:

```text
baseline cohort identity
retained identity continuity
entrants and exits
nonresponse and recontact
latent transition evidence
reported response transitions
instrument and wording versions
instrument crosswalk when measurement changes
exposure assignment timing dose and eligibility
incentive coercion and strategic-reporting state
postprocessing weighting and imputation
subgroup transition and burden
counterfactual design
post-intervention outcomes
feedback reuse
current system and validation lineage
```

A stable panel does not establish an invariant measure. An invariant measure does not identify the effect of exposure, incentive, or attrition. A changed aggregate does not identify which people changed.

## Open frontiers

The floor remains deliberately incomplete. The current frontiers include:

```text
intertemporal repeated bargaining and commitment
intersectional and unobserved-group custody
cross-organizational vendor customer and regulator succession
federated multilevel and successor authority
endogenous network and collective preference formation
real-world intervention provenance
```

Dynamic preference change is no longer listed as an unimplemented frontier. PC-10 qualifies the synthetic identification problem. A real-world dynamic-preference or performative-effect claim still requires source evidence and human review.

## Publication boundary

The floor can refuse unsupported interpretation. It cannot grant publication authority to a real-world causal, preference-change, stale-validation, unvalidated-deployment, bargaining-denial, package-imposition, agenda-suppression, manipulation, or legitimacy claim. A targeted performative path, even with observed conversion, does not establish manipulation or intent without the required counterfactual and authority evidence.
