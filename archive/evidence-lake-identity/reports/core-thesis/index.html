Working thesis · 2026-07-23

The project reconstructs how interests become infrastructure, how infrastructure becomes necessity, and how necessity becomes power.

How do private and hybrid actors shape public option sets, convert public authority into durable operating or property control, produce practical dependency or compulsion, retain residual value, and limit or preserve the public's ability to inspect, contest, substitute, or reverse the resulting system?

Post-Cold War governments externalized strategic capacity, specialist knowledge, risk, and implementation into private and hybrid institutions. Strategic ambiguity became a conversion technology: actors could move among adviser, investor, vendor, public-mission partner, infrastructure provider, and rights holder while authorship, accountability, risk, and reward remained distributed. After approximately 2011, some founder-controlled technology, defense, capital, and policy networks appear to have pursued this coordination vacuum deliberately, seeking not only to sell products but to shape missions, procurement gates, data environments, technical baselines, and institutional operating layers. The project tests when that strategy delivered public capability, when it narrowed democratic or market choice, when it produced practical dependence or forced compliance, and who retained the authority and residual value. This is a falsifiable working thesis, not a finding about every actor, estate, program, or deployment.

Timeline

Five overlapping post-Cold War phases

The phases organize context. Membership does not establish a common planner, intent, or causal relation.

1991-2001

Lean hegemon

Retain global reach and advanced capability while reducing permanent public personnel, industrial capacity, and administrative scale.

Response and inherited fog

Dominant response

  • downsizing
  • contracting out
  • defense consolidation
  • management consulting
  • privatization and public-private finance
  • globalized supply chains

Inherited fog

  • lost public technical depth
  • contractor dependence
  • concentrated supplier markets
  • capacity moved off the public organization chart

2001-2009

Expeditionary contractor state

Sustain prolonged, simultaneous, personnel-intensive missions without restoring mass mobilization or a proportionate permanent state workforce.

Response and inherited fog

Dominant response

  • contingency contracting
  • private logistics and security
  • intelligence and language contracting
  • emergency procurement
  • reconstruction and mission-support integration

Inherited fog

  • normalized emergency authorities
  • operational contractor indispensability
  • outsourced oversight
  • dispersed responsibility

2009-2016

Innovation bridge

Access commercial software, data, cloud, robotics, cyber, and venture-backed talent faster than conventional procurement and public hiring allowed.

Response and inherited fog

Dominant response

  • innovation units
  • accelerators and fellowships
  • commercial-solution solicitations
  • other transactions
  • reservist-technologists
  • government-venture bridge institutions

Inherited fog

  • bridge institutions became selectors
  • commercial availability influenced public requirements
  • capital portfolios became supplier-discovery systems
  • access to translators and sponsors became strategic

2017-2021

Dual-use platform state

Compete with peer powers while commercial firms controlled increasing shares of software, data, compute, technical labor, and manufacturing knowledge.

Response and inherited fog

Dominant response

  • national-security venture capital
  • dual-use company formation
  • software-defined defense
  • cloud and data platforms
  • rapid acquisition
  • modular and open-architecture policy
  • strategic technology portfolios

Inherited fog

  • platform dependence
  • proprietary data and interfaces
  • blurred investor-adviser-official-vendor roles
  • private architectures becoming public baselines

2022-present

Reindustrializing market-maker state

Reverse supply-chain fragility, industrial decline, and strategic dependency without directly owning most productive capacity.

Response and inherited fog

Dominant response

  • industrial subsidies
  • domestic-content and security conditions
  • milestone payments
  • strategic tariffs and export controls
  • public research consortia
  • place-based manufacturing clusters
  • allied supply-chain coordination

Inherited fog

  • firms acting as policy instruments and protected incumbents
  • public risk paired with private control rights
  • regional infrastructure folded into national-security strategy
  • unclear authorship of market-shaping choices

Persistent functions

Ten recurring archetypes

The signal is role stacking and sequence, not the mere existence of a function.

A1-convener

Convener

Creates the room, vocabulary, participant set, and legitimacy boundary before a formal decision.

A2-translator

Translator

Converts military, policy, technical, financial, legal, or civic problems into another institution's actionable language.

A3-selector

Selector or portfolio curator

Narrows which people, firms, technologies, and vehicles receive early capital, validation, access, or eligibility.

A4-public-market-maker

Public market-maker

Uses procurement, subsidy, land, infrastructure, regulation, standards, or public demand to make a private market viable.

A5-personnel-router

Personnel router

Moves tacit knowledge, trust, institutional access, and operating experience across public, private, capital, and advisory roles.

A6-paper-architect

Paper architect

Writes the contracts, rights schedules, grants, leases, deeds, standards, governance instruments, and default rules that make an arrangement durable.

A7-integrator-platform-owner

Integrator or platform owner

Assembles fragmented components into an operating system and may control interfaces, data flows, standards, accreditation, or switching costs.

A8-residual-rights-holder

Residual-rights holder

Retains the durable upside after public authority, money, infrastructure, or demand has been applied.

A9-legitimizer

Legitimizer

Supplies scientific, technical, professional, civic, or institutional credibility to the option set or installed system.

A10-adjudicator

Adjudicator

Confirms, rejects, narrows, stays, audits, reverses, or otherwise tests the lawfulness and performance of the arrangement.

Interpretation boundary

The core thesis is a research program, not a verdict. Structural opportunity, deliberate market shaping, institutional replacement, coercive governance, and personal extraction are separate propositions with separate evidence requirements. A connection, sequence, shared source, contract, ideology, or profit does not by itself prove the strongest proposition.
