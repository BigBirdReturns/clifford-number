Anduril: access, ownership, and the government gate

A finite map for reporting the company without asking the reader to understand AI. It follows two separate questions: how close a record sits to a government decision gate, and how much of the technical stack Anduril owns.

Working proposition · review required

The working reporting proposition is that Anduril has expanded ownership of an integrated defense stack while developing access to the institutions that decide eligibility, acceptance, baselines, transition authority, and ordering vehicles.

This is the question the case organizes. It is not a finding that access caused an award.

Evidence boundary

Decision-gate proximity and stack-ownership depth are separate descriptive axes and must not be read as a corruption, capture, influence, guilt, or probability score.

Only decision-specific records can establish a causal bridge.

Remote from a decision ← Decision-gate proximity → At eligibility, acceptance, baseline, or ordering
Single product or program ← Stack-ownership depth → Integrated catalog and operating layer

6 records threads

01

Army enterprise contract

Owned suite to single ordering framework

  1. Verified

    The March 2026 Army enterprise contract is a single-award, ten-year IDIQ with a $20 billion ceiling for Anduril's full commercial suite.

  2. Verified

    The Army said the enterprise contract consolidated more than 120 Anduril procurement actions into one framework.

What this does not prove
  • The $20 billion figure is a maximum ceiling, not committed revenue or obligated funds.
  • The announcement does not disclose the complete source-selection, pricing, ordering, new-item, data-rights, or substitution rules.
Document that decides the stronger question

The justification and approval, Sources Sought responses, commercial price schedule, ordering guide, acquired-product rules, data rights, and substitution provisions.

02

NGC2 common-data baseline

Integration capability to government baseline

  1. Verified

    The Army announced that Anduril leads the NGC2 common-data baseline, with Palantir retained for the Lattice-Foundry data mesh and Raft supplying registries, transformation, and federation.

What this does not prove
  • The announcement does not establish government ownership of all schemas, interfaces, deployment tools, data rights, or a completed removal-and-substitution test.
Document that decides the stronger question

The signed baseline decision, interface-control documents, responsibility matrix, data-rights schedule, acceptance record, and government-run removal test.

03

CBP autonomous towers

Lobbying scope, statutory gate, accepted product, and order

  1. Verified

    Anduril's 2025 second-quarter lobbying filing reported $700,000 in expense and named work on the Autonomous Surveillance Towers program, H.R. 1, appropriations, acquisition policy, rocket motors, connected warfare and command and control, strike, space, maritime, export controls, and foreign military sales.

  2. Verified

    Public Law 119-21 restricted specified border-technology funds to surveillance towers already tested and accepted by CBP to deliver autonomous capabilities.

  3. Verified

    CBP placed a $362,974,500 tower order on 25 December 2025 under parent IDIQ 70B02C20D00000019.

What this does not prove
  • The filing proves disclosed lobbying activity, not authorship of legislation, influence on a decision, or award causation.
  • The law does not name Anduril or establish who proposed, drafted, or intended the language.
  • The award record does not establish who authored the statutory gate, whether CBP considered no alternatives, or whether lobbying caused the order.
Document that decides the stronger question

The acceptance chronology, acquisition plan, one-offer and alternatives analyses, legislative technical-assistance record, and competitor-testing timetable.

04

Adranos and rocket motors

Acquired propulsion to public capacity support

  1. Review required

    Anduril announced that its acquisition of Adranos created its solid-rocket-motor business.

  2. Verified

    DoD announced a $14.3 million Defense Production Act Title III award for solid-rocket-motor capacity, building on a stated $75 million Anduril private investment.

What this does not prove
  • The company statement does not establish qualification, yield, accepted output, customer demand, or delivery performance.
  • Capacity support is not qualification, yield, accepted output, delivery, or a guaranteed production award.
Document that decides the stronger question

The DPA milestones, process qualification, first-pass yield, nonconformance record, accepted motors, and program-by-program production linkage.

05

Space Force SDANet

Innovation lineage to a single-award network vehicle

  1. Verified

    USAspending describes FA882024DB001 as an SBIR III follow-on single-award IDIQ for Lattice mesh networking.

  2. Review required

    Anduril describes the Space Surveillance Network path as moving from earlier innovation work into a Program of Record.

What this does not prove
  • The public record does not by itself establish government substitutability, all child-order scope, or that the entire ceiling will be used.
  • The government award, lineage, test, acceptance, price, and data-rights files control.
Document that decides the stronger question

The Phase II and III lineage, child orders, price analysis, cyber accreditation, interface ownership, sustainment competition, and replacement plan.

06

Ghost Shark control case

Visible shared-risk development to delivery

  1. Verified

    Australian Defence stated in April 2026 that Navy had taken delivery of Ghost Shark autonomous underwater vehicles.

  2. Review required

    Anduril describes Ghost Shark as a co-funded prototype-to-Program-of-Record path, making it a useful comparison with less visible sole-source or enterprise transitions.

What this does not prove
  • The release does not disclose delivery count, acceptance conditions, deficiencies, mission readiness, or availability.
  • The comparison does not establish operational success or the absence of dependence, deficiencies, or rights disputes.
Document that decides the stronger question

The co-development agreement, milestone and sea-acceptance records, delivery count, deficiencies, readiness, production yield, and government IP and substitution rights.

The two axes are an editorial reporting aperture, not a score. Lobbying, PAC activity, former-official experience, acquisitions, shared personnel, temporal sequence, and product integration do not establish influence, causation, coordination, control, wrongdoing, or unlawful procurement. A causal claim requires decision-specific communications or signed records. No wrongdoing is alleged. A ceiling is not an obligation; a prototype is not acceptance; installed capacity is not qualified output; an open interface is not government-owned substitution authority; and a source's silence is not proof of absence.
