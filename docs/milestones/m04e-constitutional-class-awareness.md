# M-04E · Constitutional class, awareness, and counterpower

M-04E converts the project’s sovereign–operator and user–represented-person arguments into a bounded, source-addressed candidate estate.

The governing unit is not a permanent label attached to a person or organization. It is:

```text
actor or institution
@ bounded system
@ time window
@ objective or decision
```

A founder may be sovereign inside a company, an operator relative to a public mission, a supplier inside a larger architecture, and a represented object relative to a regulator. No assignment travels automatically across those boundaries.

## Why “class” requires five separate axes

The first pass found that a single hierarchy is inadequate. M-04E therefore records five independent dimensions:

1. **Constitutional position** — sovereign, public host, operator, gatekeeper, worker, user, represented subject, external challenger, or counter-sovereign.
2. **Demonstrated awareness** — no demonstrated systemic awareness, local awareness, tacit constitutional awareness, explicit architectural awareness, explicit constitutional awareness, or reflexive feedback awareness.
3. **Orientation** — committed, accommodative, opportunistic, loyal-dissenting, adversarial, independent, or unknown.
4. **Material relation** — purpose rights, action authority, capital, labor, data and legibility, revenue and residual benefit, consequence burden, counterpower, legitimacy, public authority, or exit work.
5. **Effective power** — no observed institutional power, voice or petition, local operational discretion, gate/veto/collective leverage, or compulsory revision and termination.

These axes prevent several recurring category errors:

```text
position ≠ awareness
awareness ≠ alignment
alignment ≠ coordination
criticism ≠ counterpower
capital contribution ≠ sovereignty
formal public ownership ≠ practical operating control
represented status ≠ ignorance, passivity, or consent
```

## The strongest empirical finding is a source asymmetry

Wave 01 contains three represented-subject records and **zero records with direct represented-subject voice**.

The represented employee, immigration target, and patient classes are described by product documentation, institutions, or advocacy analysis. They do not speak for themselves in the current estate.

That gap is constitutionally important. The estate could otherwise reproduce the very arrangement it studies:

```text
institution defines the object
researcher receives the institution’s representation
represented person remains outside the record
```

M-04E therefore treats direct subject voice, lawful case traces, correction attempts, appeals, remedies, and collective organization as required acquisition layers rather than optional context.

## What the sample teaches

### 1. The operator–gatekeeper middle is the largest visible class

The sample contains far more operators, architects, and local action holders than formal counter-sovereigns. This is where protected purpose becomes executable:

```text
purpose
→ translation
→ eligibility
→ data model
→ access
→ workflow
→ action
```

The middle layer is not necessarily sovereign. It can nevertheless determine what becomes legible, selectable, deployable, and actionable.

### 2. Public hosts and private operators must remain separate

The Army, NHS England, and Brave1 occupy public-host or public-sovereign roles in bounded systems. Palantir, Anduril, Lockheed Martin, Mozaic, and other suppliers occupy operator, architect, worker, or exit roles.

A public procurement does not automatically transfer sovereignty. Nor does formal public data ownership prove practical control of software, interfaces, configuration, integration knowledge, or replacement.

### 3. Resource providers can be constitutionally subordinate

Palantir’s public shareholders supply capital but do not possess the founders’ reserved voting position. Fundrise supplies capital to a broad technology portfolio without demonstrated common operating purpose. Capital position and constitutional position therefore require separate ledgers.

### 4. Accommodation is not ignorance

The NHS extension record explicitly recognizes transition cost and continuity burden. The buyer retained formal authority and chose accommodation while preparing a successor procurement.

This is neither proof of capture nor proof of easy exit. It is a source-bounded class relation:

```text
formal buyer sovereignty
+ explicit dependency awareness
+ temporary accommodation
```

### 5. Worker counterpower can arise without formal governance rights

Microsoft and Google employees publicly contested the permissible objective of their labor. In the Google Maven lane, a collective challenge was followed by public AI-use principles and refusal of a follow-on contract.

The record does not establish exclusive causation or permanent employee sovereignty. It does establish that labor scarcity, resignation, collective voice, and reputational leverage can create constitutional effects without board or voting rights.

### 6. Criticism is common; compulsory revision is rare

The sample contains internal critics, external advocates, congressional oversight, an information-governance office, and a court. Only the Federal Circuit record currently demonstrates compulsory revision in the bounded sample.

M-04E therefore separates:

```text
feedback or petition
local implementation change
collective leverage
formal compulsory revision
```

### 7. The incumbent may become the exit operator

The NHS transition-and-exit contract places the incumbent supplier inside the mechanism intended to move products away from the incumbent environment.

That is not proof of bad faith. It reveals a recurring constitutional problem: the institution may depend on the operator’s knowledge to make substitution possible.
