- direct represented-person voice and retrospective proof burden;

## What changed

### Direct voice is no longer absent

Wave 01 contained no direct represented-person voice. Wave 02 adds one source-bounded direct-subject record while preserving the much larger deployment-specific voice deficit.

The new result is:

```text
direct represented-person voice records: 1
```

That record cannot be generalized to every represented population or deployment.

### Counterpower is now observed in another bounded lane

The SEVIS order supplies a second kind of positive control alongside the DCGS-A2 procurement case:

```text
agency action
→ subject litigation
→ independent court review
→ restoration and prospective restraint
```

The order does not establish a universal administrative appeal, national practice change, final merits judgment, or Palantir role.

### NHS feedback is separated from veto

Official governance minutes now preserve:

- aggregated public questions and feedback;
- direct Patient and Public Voices participation;
- challenges to implied consent, live-data use, AI validation, trust, complaints, portability, and use-case definition;
- programme actions and reported communication or steering changes;
- scrutiny of waiting-list and savings attribution.

These records support observed responsiveness and prospective challenge. They do not yet establish that the group or affected patients could compel a stay, reverse a consequence, or redefine the programme objective.

### Palantir governance has an operated counterfactual surface

The 2026 proxy and annual-meeting record establish:

- two shareholder-proposed alternative governance instruments;
- board opposition and formal consideration;
- a formula-based Class F voting privilege;
- actual defeat of both proposals;
- a documented dispute over independent access to customer and deployment evidence.

The records do not establish that founder votes alone caused the result or how a one-share-one-vote counterfactual would have resolved.

### Human presence is narrowed to an inspectable claim

A Brave1 first-party record states that an operator selects the target and may cancel the attack at any time in a highly automated interceptor workflow.

That supports acquisition of:

```text
interface
confidence state
reaction window
operator action
abort log
failed engagement
human-factors test
```

It does not by itself prove meaningful veto in practice.

### Worker criticism remains below compulsory power

Worker-authored sources preserve direct warnings about:

- non-public risk information;
- confidentiality and retaliation;
- protected reporting and public disclosure;
- collective withdrawal from a named government-cloud project.

They establish voice, proposed preventive rights, and a named non-participation counterfactual. They do not establish binding worker veto, contract termination, or exclusive causation.

## Non-links and controls

Wave 02 adds an explicit non-link:

```text
SEVIS termination and consequence
≠ current evidence of Palantir, ICM, or ImmigrationOS involvement
```

That comparator remains useful for notice, burden, custody, consequence, and remedy analysis without being imported into the Palantir lane.

Other controls remain intact:

- NGC2 retains programme-level competition and alternate implementation leads;
- NHS retains public data-control and multi-supplier evidence;
- Brave1 remains publicly directed at the programme level;
- shareholder proposals demonstrate some formal counterfactual access even where voting power is unequal.

## Remaining lake gaps

The highest-value missing objects are now narrower:

1. A deployment-specific Palantir or ImmigrationOS person-level inference-to-consequence chain.
2. Complete ICE source-system, interface, log, notice, and administrative-record custody.
3. Observed implementation and institutional effect of OIG stopping recommendations.
4. NHS terms of reference, authority matrix, challenge outcomes, patient-level consequence, and observed stay or reversal.
5. Palantir proposal vote reconstruction by class and beneficial holder, board deliberations, and peer governance comparators.
6. Brave1 failed-validation, incident, recall, casualty, compensation, IP, revenue, and remedy denominator.
7. Worker warning outcomes: retaliation, board response, regulator access, work refusal, staffing effect, and deployment change.
8. One complete error-to-learning-to-residual-benefit chain with the affected party and remedy identified.
