## Public demand and private revenue are now joined

Palantir's 2025 Form 10-K reports:

- $2.402 billion in government revenue;
- $1.576 billion in government-segment contribution at a 66 percent contribution margin;
- 54 percent of total revenue from government customers;
- $1.9 billion in U.S. government revenue, up from $1.2 billion in 2024.

That supports a material public-demand-to-private-revenue flow. It does not identify award-level margin, the profit attributable to a challenged gate, or disproportionate private benefit.

## Founder control is explicit

Palantir's filings describe a multi-class capital structure, Founder Voting Trust, and Founder Voting Agreement that generally allow the founders and certain affiliates to control up to 49.999999 percent of voting power, with other founder and affiliate holdings capable of adding voting power.

This supplies exact residual-control architecture. It does not show that public contracts created that control or that founders personally shaped a particular benefiting award.

## Rights are mixed rather than simply privatized

The Army and ICE records describe a divided rights structure:

```text
Government data and derived information
  → Government ownership or broad rights

Palantir commercial platform, software, source code and documentation
  → private proprietary rights
```

That allocation can produce real transition and substitution questions. It does not by itself establish disproportionate private rights, loss of public data, or impossible exit.

## Formal counterpower exists

Palantir's 10-K reports that government contracts often preserve rights to terminate for convenience, reduce or modify orders, reduce prices for defective cost or pricing data, audit or investigate, impose penalties, and suspend or debar contractors.

These are material counterweights and evidence against a claim of complete private control. Their practical effectiveness remains an empirical question requiring clause-level and outcome records.

## T3 clears a partial threshold

The bounded Palantir lane now contains:

```text
public demand
→ material company revenue and segment contribution
→ retained proprietary platform rights
→ durable founder governance
```

Public data rights and formal government remedies coexist with that cycle.

The missing T3 return path is:

```text
public-derived private capacity
→ renewed lobbying, litigation, standards, procurement advocacy,
  institutional design, company formation or other gate-shaping action
```

The full organism metabolism test therefore remains incomplete even though the partial company-level cycle is supported.

## I5 remains unproved for a much narrower reason

The current estate has the ingredients:

- a bounded procurement-gate intervention;
- material government revenue;
- founder governance and beneficial ownership;
- private platform rights;
- formal public remedies.

It lacks the causal benefit ledger:

- award-level obligations, outlays, direct costs, margin and performance;
- public risk and alternative-provider comparison;
- founder-specific vesting, sales, compensation and realized-benefit chronology;
- conflicts, recusals, board decisions and related-party records;
- proof that a founder materially shaped the gate producing the specific benefit;
- evidence that the resulting gain was disproportionate to value delivered and private risk borne;
- recovery and clawback outcomes.

So:

```text
private benefit architecture: documented
personal extraction: not established
```

## The core corporate architecture is not hidden

SEC filings publicly disclose founder control, government-revenue dependence, ownership structure, government-contract risks and remedies. Procurement records disclose part of the data and platform-rights allocation.

That closes the broad claim that core corporate control and government dependence are concealed.

The narrower T7 proposition remains open: whether end-to-end operating responsibility, interfaces, consequences, performance and transition are fragmented across entities, contracts, systems and redactions in a way that prevents reconstruction.

## Next exact acquisitions

### Award-level economics

- legal recipient and parent;
- obligation, outlay and recognized revenue;
- direct and allocated cost;
- contract-level margin;
- performance and acceptance;
- refund, recovery, termination and renewal;
- competitor and public-capacity controls.

### Founder benefit and decision chronology

- beneficial ownership by date;
- options, RSUs, vesting, exercises and sales;
- voting-trust changes;
- compensation and board approvals;
- conflicts and recusals;
- public-gate actions and dates;
- causal attribution to specific contract-derived benefit.

### Gate-feedback cycle

Trace whether government-derived revenue or platform position financed or enabled later:

- bid protests and litigation;
- lobbying and procurement advocacy;
- standards and policy participation;
- government-entry infrastructure;
- investment, company formation or personnel routing;
- additional sole-source or enterprise gates.

### Counterpower in practice

Acquire actual instances of:

- termination or reduction;
- modification;
- price recovery;
- audit findings;
- suspension or debarment review;
- platform replacement;
- data and ontology migration;
- continued public operation after exit.
