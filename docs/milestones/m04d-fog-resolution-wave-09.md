# M-04D fog-resolution wave 09

Wave 09 resolves practical-exit and substitution fog in bounded UK health and social-care Palantir lanes. It distinguishes an awarded alternate-supplier product transition, material transition burdens, a formal exit contract, a competitively retained incumbent, public data-control provisions, and a differentiated supplier ecosystem.

## One real alternate-supplier transition instrument exists

The Department of Health and Social Care awarded Mozaic Services Limited a £100,000 contract to transition the Adult Social Care Dashboard from Palantir Foundry to EDGE between August 16 and September 30, 2021.

This establishes:

```text
named product
+ incumbent platform
+ receiving platform
+ different transition supplier
+ bounded period and price
```

It does not prove that the migration completed, users cut over, data validated, operations continued, or residual Palantir dependencies disappeared.

## Transition burden is documented

NHS England stated in 2022 that changing contractor before the successor procurement would cause significant inconvenience and substantial duplicated cost and would be impractical and uneconomic.

It later awarded Palantir a separate 12-month, £24.925 million transition and exit contract for critical COVID-19 and elective-recovery products.

These records support C5 at a bounded level:

```text
incumbent operational products
→ continuity concern
→ extension
→ funded transition and exit work
```

They do not establish that replacement was impossible, that the costs were independently validated, or that Palantir caused the burden improperly.

## The successor procurement was competitive—but retained the incumbent

The FDP award notice records:

- competitive dialogue;
- seven tenders received;
- quality weighted at 60 percent;
- social value at 10 percent;
- price at 30 percent;
- award to Palantir Technologies UK, Ltd.

Therefore the proposition that the transition process produced a different core platform supplier is a bounded non-link.

This result does not determine whether incumbent advantage affected the process, whether the new platform is materially different, or whether other suppliers could have delivered it successfully.

## Formal public data-control rights exist

The FDP procurement notice states that the platform will be owned and controlled by the NHS. The National Data Guardian states that the supplier will not control the data and is not permitted to access, use, or share it for its own purposes, and that use was bounded to the stated use cases absent further public engagement.

These are substantive public-control provisions. They do not prove practical portability, public ownership of source code or ontology, effective audit, or successful enforcement.

## The broader program is multi-supplier

Official procurement notices separately identify:

- Palantir for the core FDP platform;
- IQVIA for privacy-enhancing technology;
- KPMG for technical support and implementation;
- Kainos for a Population Health Management product-development role.

That closes the literal claim that one supplier performs every material FDP function.

The decisive question becomes actual dependence rather than nominal supplier count:

- Who controls each interface?
- Which modules can operate independently?
- Who approves deployment?
- Who owns configuration, ontology, and derivative work?
- Which supplier can be replaced without Palantir participation?

## Practical enterprise exit remains unresolved

The current record is deliberately mixed:

```text
alternate-supplier product transition contract
+ formal platform exit contract
+ competitive successor procurement
+ public data-control provisions
+ differentiated suppliers

versus

material transition cost
+ incumbent continuity at the core platform
+ no observed enterprise migration or replacement record
```

Neither complete portability nor complete lock-in is established.
