## An actual post-opening Erebor security is now in custody

Fundrise Innovation Fund's audited annual report lists:

- **issuer:** Erebor Bank, N.A.;
- **security:** Series B preferred stock;
- **initial acquisition:** February 20, 2026;
- **reported cost:** $5 million;
- **reported fair value:** $5 million at March 31, 2026;
- **portfolio weight:** 0.7 percent of fund net assets;
- **classification:** restricted, non-income-producing, Level 3 investment.

This is a transaction-specific capital record. It does not disclose the complete round, ownership percentage, board rights, vetoes, information rights, side letters, allocation, or use of proceeds.

## The chronology is exact but not causal

The FDIC records an Erebor opening date of February 6, 2026. Fundrise reports its Series B acquisition on February 20, 2026.

That supplies:

```text
public operating gate
→ fourteen days
→ private equity acquisition
```

The timing does not establish favoritism, regulator influence, satisfaction of a capital condition, or causation. Those questions require the financing authorization, capital plan, closing conditions, investor allocation, regulatory correspondence, and matched post-opening de novo financings.

## Exclusive aligned capital does not survive

Fundrise Advisors, LLC is disclosed as the fund's SEC-registered investment adviser and a subsidiary of Rise Companies Corp. It is not part of the current M-04B named aligned-capital set.

Therefore the proposition that Erebor's post-opening equity was supplied exclusively by the currently named founder, defense-technology, and aligned-venture network is a bounded non-link at the present waterline.

That result does not prove complete independence, absence of syndication, or absence of currently named investors in the same round.

## Co-portfolio exposure is a useful control

The same audited schedule reports positions in Erebor, Anduril, SpaceX, and many unrelated technology companies through direct securities, special-purpose vehicles, and co-investment structures.

This is an ordinary explanation for shared-investor topology:

```text
diversified innovation-fund mandate
→ co-holdings across technology and defense companies
```

Co-holding establishes common portfolio custody. It does not establish common operating design, intercompany transactions, shared governance, or coordination.

The decisive comparison is now between:

- ordinary broad innovation funds;
- concentrated or thematic defense funds;
- direct securities versus SPVs and co-investment vehicles;
- passive minority holdings versus governance, information, priority, and transaction rights.

## Security ownership still does not establish control

The Fundrise report identifies a security and general proxy-voting policy. It does not disclose Erebor-specific:

- voting power;
- board or observer seats;
- protective provisions;
- information rights;
- pro rata or follow-on rights;
- rights of first refusal or co-sale;
- side letters;
- exercised proxy decisions.

Those rights require the Series B purchase and governance instruments.

## Silent Ventures remains a vehicle-and-rights problem

The existing Form D identifies Silent Ventures Fund II, its management company, GP, upper-tier GP, manager, exemptions, and pre-first-sale filing state. Silent Ventures' site claims Erebor and Traysar as portfolio companies.

The sources do not establish which Silent fund, SPV, syndicate, partner, or affiliate made either investment, or the security, date, amount, ownership, board, veto, information, follow-on, exit, or write-off state.

A portfolio listing remains a first-party acquisition route—not a transaction or control record.

## Erebor's proposed banking-organ function still lacks traffic

The estate now contains:

- charter and deposit-insurance records;
- opening chronology;
- stated technology, payments, investment, defense, and digital-asset customer focus;
- named organizers and principal shareholder;
- capital and supervisory conditions;
- one external Series B investor;
- portfolio adjacency.

It does not contain an actual named:

- depositor;
- borrower;
- payment counterparty;
- treasury customer;
- custody relationship;
- credit facility;
- pricing term;
- default;
- transaction volume.

The proposed Erebor ecosystem-banking organ therefore remains below threshold.
