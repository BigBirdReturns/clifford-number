## Palantir's origin is a filed company claim, not independent operational proof

Palantir's SEC filings state that the company was founded in 2003 to build software for the U.S. intelligence community's counterterrorism work, released Gotham in 2008 for intelligence customers, and later expanded into broader government and commercial functions and additional platforms.

That chronology is relevant and source-bounded as a securities filing. It still requires independent early contract, customer, mission, product-version, performance, and failure records.

## The Army has an independent platform lineage

The Army describes DCGS-A as a quick-reaction intelligence capability used extensively in Iraq and Afghanistan before approval for service-wide deployment in 2012.

That is a public-institution transition:

```text
expeditionary quick-reaction capability
→ combat use
→ acquisition approval
→ globally networked service platform
```

It is serious counterevidence to a claim that a founder-controlled private network originated the entire integrated-intelligence trajectory.

## Identity intelligence moved beyond its wartime roots

The Army's Biometrically Enabled Intelligence record describes:

- collection of fingerprints, iris scans, faces, DNA and other identity data;
- high-confidence identity resolution;
- fusion with all-source intelligence;
- watchlist dissemination;
- employment denial, questioning and targeting support;
- Iraq and Afghanistan use;
- subsequent use against enduring and emerging threats and in other geographic commands.

This supports a bounded two-sided method transfer from wartime identity intelligence into broader missions. It does not establish Palantir involvement or transfer into ICE, CBP, or another civilian system.

## One Palantir Army sequence is now visible

The record supports an actor-specific sequence:

```text
DCGS-A2 commercial-alternative gate challenge
→ later Army intelligence and targeting prototype competition
→ TITAN selection
→ Foundry integration inside the NGC2 data mesh
```

That is a meaningful P3-to-P5 company trajectory. It does not prove that the DCGS litigation caused the later selections or that the same product, code, ontology, data, or personnel crossed each boundary.

## Functional lineage is stronger than exact transfer

Palantir's filing describes a counterterror pattern:

```text
integrate intelligence sources
→ identify hidden patterns
→ hand analysis to operational users
→ execute real-world responses
→ receive operational feedback
```

Later Army records describe Palantir roles in AI-enabled sensor processing and targeting through TITAN and in a Foundry-based edge-to-cloud command data mesh through NGC2.

That supports review of a functional lineage. The repository still lacks the exact transferred object:

- product and version;
- source code and architecture;
- ontology and model;
- data schema and migration;
- engineering team and personnel;
- license and rights;
- Government requirement and acceptance lineage.

## Counterterror-to-ICE transfer remains unproved

The estate now contains:

- a filer-reported counterterror product origin;
- ICE ICM and ImmigrationOS contracts and deployment;
- broad functional similarities in identity, data joining, prioritization, workflow and operational support.

It does not contain a two-sided transfer record identifying what moved from a counterterror platform into ICE, who moved it, through which contract or architecture, when the receiving system changed, or what consequence followed.

Therefore BRG-03 remains `requires_additional_acquisition`.

## The system-wide origin claim narrows

The Army's DCGS and identity-intelligence records establish public-system origins and institutional transfer paths independent of any one company. The Palantir record establishes a company-specific counterterror origin claim and later Army expansion.

The strongest supported formulation is now:

> Public institutions and commercial vendors jointly and competitively developed overlapping intelligence, identity, targeting and command architectures after 2001. Palantir reports a counterterror origin and later obtained important roles in Army intelligence, targeting and command programs. Exact product and method transfers can be tested program by program.

The record does not support:

> One founder-controlled private network originated and continuously controlled the full architecture.
