## P2 is now independently receipted

GAO documented extensive contractor reliance in Iraq and Afghanistan. For the reviewed period, the agencies reported at least $33.9 billion in obligations across almost 57,000 contracts, and DOD reported 197,718 contractor personnel as of April 2008. GAO simultaneously documented incomplete, unreliable, or absent contractor-personnel and casualty tracking.

That record supports the expeditionary-contractor state as an institution-level historical condition. It does not identify one contractor or network as its designer or establish that emergency opacity was intentional.

## P3 is a formal institutional bridge

GAO's DIU review states that DOD created DIU in 2015 to accelerate commercial-technology adoption and that DIU established a flexible award process. From fiscal years 2016 through 2023, DIU made 450 prototype awards and reported 62 production transitions, representing 51 percent of projects with at least one completed prototype.

The correct inference is bounded:

```text
commercial problem and supplier universe
→ public selector and translator
→ prototype agreement
→ possible production transition
```

Complete applicant, nonselected, nontransition, user, performance, investor, and obligation denominators remain required before testing recurring-network advantage.

## Flexible acquisition expanded across P3 and P4

GAO found that DOD's use of prototype other transactions increased significantly from fiscal years 2016 through 2018 and that nontraditional companies participated to a significant extent in 88 percent of the transactions awarded during that period. GAO also identified review-control failures in two of eleven transactions it examined.

This supports acquisition-mechanism diffusion. It does not prove capture, improper awards generally, or production success.

## A bounded Palantir I2→I3 sequence survives

The combined record now supports review of a longitudinal sequence:

```text
DCGS-A2 commercial-item gate challenge
→ mandatory statutory reconsideration before award
→ later competitive Army deployments
→ 2025 enterprise purchasing framework
→ J&A-described proprietary integration and dependence
```

The sequence does not prove that the litigation caused later enterprise dependence. The intervening solicitation, evaluation, acceptance, performance, competition, modification, and operating records must establish or defeat that causal bridge.

## The direct ICM→risk-classification join does not survive

The Second Circuit's description of the ICE agency record identifies the Risk Classification Assessment module as part of the Enforcement Case Tracking System stored in the Enforcement Integrated Database. It does not identify ICM as the RCA module or system.

Therefore:

```text
Palantir ICM is the ICE RCA system
→ bounded_non_link
```

The opinion also describes a broader event-centric ICE data architecture:

```text
EID common repository
+ ECTS/RCA module
+ IIDS reporting snapshot
+ shared A-Number relational key
```

That architecture can link apprehension, risk-assessment, detention, removal, and bond data to people. But no current record identifies a material ICM-derived field, analytic output, alert, or workflow input used by RCA.

The remaining hypothesis is narrower:

```text
ICM data or analytics
→ unknown interface
→ ECTS/EID RCA workflow
```

That requires interface-control documents, field lineage, shared-identifier use, audit logs, role rules, and consequence-specific case traces.

## The historical thesis is stronger—but not the strongest capture claim

The combined official record now supports the five phases as an institution-level periodization:

```text
P1 reduced acquisition capacity and contractor reliance
→ P2 expeditionary contractor scale
→ P3 commercial innovation bridge
→ P4 flexible acquisition and platform scaling
→ P5 speed-and-scale market-making institutions
```

What remains unproved is a single continuous actor-specific design across those phases. One Palantir I2→I3 sequence cannot establish founder-network control over P1, P2, or the entire public-private architecture.
