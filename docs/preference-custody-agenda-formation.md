# Preference custody: agenda formation and collective option generation

This candidate-only laboratory supports issue [#594](https://github.com/BigBirdReturns/clifford-number/issues/594). It closes one additional identification boundary: a response among institution-authored options does not reveal every option an affected public could generate, nor whether the public accepts the objective being optimized.

The fixture is synthetic. It creates no graph effect, thesis evidence, real-world causal conclusion, legitimacy verdict, suppression finding, preference-change finding, manipulation claim, or inference of intent.

## Frozen population

The fixture contains 1,000 people in two equal affected groups. Every cohort has a complete ranking over:

```text
A
B
C
NONE
```

The institution initially offers only A and B. Under that forced binary choice, the population produces:

```text
A = 800
B = 200
A share = 80%
```

The same population has a different complete first-choice distribution:

```text
C = 800
A = 100
NONE = 100
B = 0
```

A separate objective disposition records:

```text
accept objective = 400
reject objective = 600
```

No preference changes during the fixture. The variation comes from the agenda rights and decision rule.

## Four agenda worlds

### Fixed commissioner agenda

The commissioner authors A and B, accepts no public proposal or challenge, and implements A from the preliminary 80/20 result.

### Advisory collective proposal

An attributed cross-group coalition proposes C with support from 800 people, including 400 from each affected group. The process permits suggestions but gives them no binding amendment consequence. C is not added and A remains the implemented result.

### Binding collective amendment

The same proposal is evaluated under a binding affected-public instrument. The instrument preserves option proposal, coalition formation, agenda amendment, objective challenge, appeal, and remedy. C clears both the overall petition threshold and the threshold within each affected group.

The ballot expands to A, B, C, and NONE. C receives 800 first choices and becomes the implemented option.

### Binding objective challenge

The affected public invokes the right to challenge the objective itself. Six hundred people reject the frame. The binding rejection threshold passes, no option is selected, and implementation is blocked.

## What the control proves

The preliminary 80 percent support for A is compatible with three different consequences:

```text
A implemented under a fixed agenda
C implemented after binding public option generation
the objective rejected and implementation blocked
```

The difference is not a change in preference. It is the authority to propose an option, form a coalition, amend the agenda, challenge the objective, and bind implementation.

The executable refusal rules are:

```text
forced choice ≠ complete agenda
unoffered option may be collectively generated
proposal without a binding rule ≠ agenda authority
preference among options ≠ acceptance of the objective
preliminary winner ≠ final public disposition
collective option generation requires attributed membership and rule
synthetic prediction cannot exercise amendment rights
binding objective rejection blocks implementation
```

## Custody chain

Every agenda world emits a SHA-256 hash-linked chain:

```text
population and rankings sealed
→ initial agenda authored
→ preliminary forced choice observed
→ public agenda action recorded
→ agenda authority instrument sealed
→ agenda rule resolved
→ final ballot or objective disposition recorded
→ implementation resolved
→ interpretation sealed
```

The preliminary response cannot overwrite the complete ranking. The advisory proposal cannot acquire binding authority through popularity alone. The binding amendment and objective rejection must preserve their membership, thresholds, group support, disposition, and implementation consequence.

## Real-case evidence floor

A real deployment must preserve:

```text
initial question and option set
complete considered, excluded, and never-generated alternatives
proposal origin and attributed membership
coalition formation and support by group
amendment and objective-challenge rules
preliminary and final ballots
attributed agenda disposition
implementation or blocking receipt
```

A synthetic audience may identify an option that human teams had not considered. It remains a candidate generator. It cannot become the affected constituency, form a real coalition, exercise amendment rights, reject an objective, or authorize implementation.

## Promotion boundary

This control does not establish that Electric Twin, News UK, Dentsu, any vendor, any publisher, or any named institution suppressed an alternative or denied an objective challenge. A real case needs an observable option-generation process, attributed participants, a governing rule, a recorded disposition, and a consequence that the disposition was permitted to bind.
