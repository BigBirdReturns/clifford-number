# Choice architecture, exit authorship, assent, and payment-sequence custody

PC-22 separates verified comprehension and a recorded choice disposition from the architecture, intention, authorship, assent, payment sequence, and reversal state that produced the record. The control holds comprehension, the operative release, affected population, final recorded disposition, payment population, payment amount, and public status constant while changing the interface and agency mechanism beneath them.

The fixture is synthetic. It creates no graph effect, thesis evidence, named-interface audit, consent finding, coercion finding, manipulation conclusion, enforceability opinion, public-authorization verdict, legitimacy finding, or inference of intent.

## Frozen state

```text
technical correction          complete
final reference proposal      A1

verified comprehension        100 people
public choice status          choice_final
affected people               100
formal choices recorded       100
recorded bound                 95

```

Every world therefore presents the same institutional record:

```text
100 people understood the operative consequence
100 choice records exist
95 are recorded as bound
5 are recorded as exited
100 received payment
status is choice_final
```

That record does not identify how the interface converted intention into the final disposition.

## Eight architecture and agency systems

### Neutral symmetric active choice before payment

The positive control uses no default, balanced option order, equal salience, two steps and one confirmation on both paths, identical authentication, zero documentary or monetary asymmetry, a sixty-day window, and payment only after choice.

```text
intended exits                  5
attempted exits                 5
completed exits                 5
self-authored choices         100
representative substitutions    0
default or automated records    0
explicit assents               95
inferred assents                0
cooling-off window             14 days
reversals completed             2
```

The interface, authorship, explanation, objection, appeal, re-administration, and correction ledgers are independently complete. This is the one qualified neutral, self-authored, pre-payment, and meaningfully reversible path.

### Binding by default

The second world records nonresponse as binding. Twenty people intend to exit, but only five attempt and complete the route. Eighty records arise through the default rather than an active person-authored choice.

```text
intended exits                 20
completed exits                 5
suppressed exit intentions     15
default or automated records   80
explicit assents               15
inferred assents               80
```

The final 95/5 record is identical to the positive world even though most bound records are default-generated.

### Exit authentication and cost asymmetry

The binding path requires one step, an account login, no documentation, and no monetary cost. The exit path requires five steps, notarized mail, photo identification, a notarized form, and a 25-unit cost.

```text
intended exits                 15
attempted exits                12
completed exits                 5
failed exits                    7
abandoned exits                 3
```

The exit option is visible. Its existence does not establish a symmetric or usable path.

### Urgent countdown and short deadline

The fourth world preserves equal formal path length but imposes a one-day deadline, a high-countdown state, and an expiring banner.

```text
intended exits                 12
attempted exits                 8
completed exits                 5
failed exits                    3
abandoned exits                 4
```

The process records a completed choice for every person while converting unresolved or incomplete action into the same final disposition.

### Repeated-confirmation asymmetry

The binding path requires one confirmation. Exit requires four confirmations.

```text
intended exits                 10
attempted exits                 6
completed exits                 5
failed exits                    1
abandoned exits                 4
```

Nominally equal option visibility coexists with unequal completion burden.

### Assent bundled with payment access

The sixth world allows affected people to receive the payment only through a nonseverable release-and-payment transaction. Every recorded choice is person-authored, and ninety-five explicit assent receipts exist, but no independent release choice is available.

```text
bundled benefit                PAYMENT-ACCESS
independent choice             false
severable                      false
self-authored choices          100
explicit assent receipts        95
independent assent qualified   false
```

Authorship and explicit action do not by themselves establish independent assent when the release is bundled with access to the remedy.

### Representative or operator substitution

The seventh world preserves complete comprehension but allows a representative or call-center operator to record thirty choices without affected-person ratification.

```text
self-authored choices           70
representative substitutions    30
explicit assents                65
inferred assents                30
reversal attempts                5
reversals completed              0
```

A complete administrative record does not identify whose choice the record contains.

### Payment before choice with clawback exit

The final world pays all 100 people before the choice process. Retaining payment is treated as assent, and exit requires repayment confirmation, two confirmations, and an 18-unit cost. Ninety-five people are exposed to clawback.

```text
intended exits                 20
attempted exits                 8
completed exits                 5
failed exits                    3
abandoned exits                12
payment before choice         100 people
clawback exposure              95 people
explicit assents               83
inferred assents               12
```

Payment sequence, bundling, path cost, urgency, and confirmation asymmetry coexist under the same 95/5 public disposition.

## Choice is a process object

PC-22 distinguishes at least six objects that are often collapsed:

```text
operative understanding
latent intention
attempted action
completed action
recorded disposition
binding authority
```

A person may understand the choice and intend to exit while failing to complete the path. A system may then record binding by default. The resulting administrative record is not a measurement of latent intention unless the path and fallback mechanism are preserved.

## Interface asymmetry

A visible pair of options is insufficient. The control requires custody for:

```text
number of steps
authentication method
documentary burden
monetary cost
device and channel availability
time to completion
deadline and extension
confirmation count
error and abandonment state
fallback disposition
```

Two labels on one screen can conceal materially different completion functions.

## Authorship and assent

PC-22 separates the actor who generated a record from the person to whom the record is attributed.

```text
person-authored choice
representative-authored choice
operator-authored choice
default-generated record
automated fallback record
```

It also separates explicit action from independent assent. A person can explicitly click a bundled transaction without receiving an independent opportunity to accept the benefit and reject the release.

## Payment sequence and reversal

The payment ledger must preserve when payment occurred relative to notice, comprehension, choice, assent, and binding. Payment before choice can create a repayment or clawback condition that changes the practical exit path.

A cooling-off label is also insufficient. Usable reconsideration requires:

```text
window duration
notice of the window
reversal route
attempt receipt
completed reversal receipt
payment and clawback consequence
re-administration
corrected final disposition
```

The positive and default worlds both retain meaningful reversal receipts. The representative-substitution world nominally offers reversal but completes none of five attempts, so it does not qualify.

## Real-case promotion boundary

A real choice-architecture claim requires:

```text
affected and comprehending population identity
interface identity, code, version, deployment, and capture hash
default, preselection, order, salience, and framing state
active-choice requirement and nonresponse fallback
bind and exit path steps, authentication, documentation, time, cost, device, and channel
urgency, deadline, countdown, reminders, extensions, and waivers
confirmation and repeated-confirmation state
latent intention instrument and uncertainty
attempt, failure, abandonment, and completion receipts
authorship identity and ratification
explicit and inferred assent treatment
benefit bundling and severability
payment timing, repayment, clawback, recoupment, and retention treatment
cooling-off, reversal, re-administration, and correction
interface exposure, assignment, telemetry, and audit
explanation, objection, appeal, durability, and authority
```

## Custody chain

Each world emits a SHA-256 hash-linked chain:

```text
population, comprehension, release, payment, and public status
→ default, order, salience, path cost, deadline, and confirmation
→ intention, attempt, completion, failure, abandonment, and authorship
→ explicit, inferred, representative, default, and bundled assent
→ payment sequence, clawback, cooling-off, reversal, and re-administration
→ audit, explanation, objection, appeal, correction, and authority
→ mechanism classification and suppressed-intention consequence
→ interpretation seal
```

Mutating the default, authentication, cost, deadline, confirmation, intention, authorship, assent, bundling, payment, clawback, reversal, governance, classification, or interpretation breaks the chain.

## Publication boundary

The admissible laboratory statement is that verified comprehension, one operative release, one public `choice_final` status, one recorded 95-bound/5-exited disposition, and full payment can coexist with neutral active choice, binding default, asymmetric exit cost, urgency, repeated confirmation, bundled assent, representative substitution, or payment-first clawback. A real choice-architecture claim requires complete interface, intention, authorship, assent, payment, reversal, validation, correction, durability, and authority custody.
