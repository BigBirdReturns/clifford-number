# Federated liability, loss allocation, insurance, and public-remedy custody

PC-16 separates complete technical recovery from contractual allocation, insurance response, direct affected-party standing, actual payment, enforcement, and durable public remedy. The control holds the incident, six-organization topology, final A1 state, public `recovered` status, affected population, and 3,500-unit loss constant while changing who can claim, who must pay, who actually pays, how long enforcement takes, and which loss remains externalized.

The fixture is synthetic. It creates no graph effect, thesis evidence, legal opinion, insurance-coverage determination, damages finding, named-organization allegation, public-authorization verdict, or inference of breach, misconduct, negligence, causation, or intent.

## Frozen incident ledger

```text
organizations                    6
technical recovery               complete
final reference proposal         A1
public incident status           recovered
affected people                  100
direct affected-party harm       2,000 units
consequential institutional      1,000 units
technical recovery cost            500 units
total loss                       3,500 units
```

Every world preserves this technical and loss state.

## Eight liability and remedy systems

### Complete joint allocation, insurance, and paid public remedy

The complete world preserves coordinated contribution among the contracting organizations, a resolved primary payer, direct affected-party standing, an accepted insurance claim, a supplemental public fund, a single enforceable forum, complete payment of all 3,500 units, complete compensation of the 2,000-unit affected-party harm, and a remedy that survives organizational succession.

```text
insurance payment              1,500
organization payments          1,400
public fund                       600
total paid                     3,500
affected-party compensation   2,000
uncompensated affected harm        0
externalized loss                  0
```

This is the fixture's one complete governance-and-remedy state.

### Vendor indemnity protects the customer only

The vendor pays 1,500 units under an indemnity running only to the customer. Affected people have no direct contractual standing, no collective route, and receive no compensation.

```text
contractual payment            1,500
affected-party compensation        0
uncompensated affected harm    2,000
externalized loss              2,000
```

An indemnity between organizations is not a direct public remedy.

### Liability cap externalizes the balance

Affected people have standing and obtain the full amount permitted by a 500-unit cap. The cap is enforced, but it leaves 1,500 units of direct affected-party harm uncompensated and 3,000 units of total loss externalized.

```text
liability cap                    500
total paid                       500
uncompensated affected harm    1,500
externalized loss              3,000
```

An enforceable cap can resolve the payment amount without producing complete compensation.

### Circular mutual indemnities

The vendor, cloud provider, customer, and downstream organization each point to another contract. No primary payer or contribution allocation resolves, the forum clauses conflict, and no payment occurs.

```text
primary payer                   unresolved
contribution                    unresolved
total paid                              0
uncompensated affected harm         2,000
externalized loss                   3,500
```

Contractual density is not accountable allocation.

### Upstream disclaimer concentrates loss on the customer

Affected people receive the full 2,000-unit compensation and the complete 3,500-unit loss is paid, but every unit comes from the customer because upstream organizations disclaimed responsibility.

```text
customer payment               3,500
affected-party compensation   2,000
externalized loss                  0
```

Complete affected-party compensation does not prove correct cross-organizational allocation. The remedy is also not durable after customer succession or insolvency.

### Insurance exclusion defeats expected recovery

A 3,500-unit policy exists. The insurer applies an exclusion, pays nothing, and the customer absorbs only a 500-unit amount. Affected people receive nothing.

```text
policy limit                   3,500
insurance paid                    0
customer payment                 500
uncompensated affected harm    2,000
externalized loss              3,000
```

Policy existence is distinct from coverage, claim acceptance, and payment.

### Fragmented forum delays payment

Affected people have standing, but the vendor, cloud provider, customer, insurer, courts, and arbitration clauses route through different proceedings. After 365 days, only 1,000 units have been paid and only 500 units reached affected people.

```text
enforcement delay                365 days
total paid                     1,000
affected-party compensation     500
uncompensated affected harm    1,500
externalized loss              2,500
```

A formally available forum does not establish timely or complete remedy.

### Causation burden blocks the public fund

Affected people have formal standing and an appeal route. The public compensation fund applies a strict causation standard and denies payment. The customer makes a 250-unit interim payment, none of which compensates the affected population.

```text
public fund paid                   0
interim customer payment         250
affected-party compensation        0
uncompensated affected harm    2,000
externalized loss              3,250
```

Standing and technical correction do not guarantee that the evidentiary burden for compensation can be satisfied.

## Contract, coverage, adjudication, and payment are separate objects

PC-16 preserves the following sequence as distinct transactions:

```text
contract language
→ liability trigger
→ notice
→ standing
→ causation burden
→ claim or adjudication
→ coverage or exclusion
→ enforceable allocation
→ actual payment
→ affected-party compensation
→ contribution and subrogation
→ residual externalized loss
```

A valid contract does not prove that its trigger was met. An accepted claim does not prove full indemnification. A judgment does not prove collection. A customer payment does not prove the correct upstream allocation.

## Affected-party compensation and system allocation

Two worlds fully compensate affected people. Only one also resolves the broader organizational allocation and preserves a durable remedy.

```text
complete affected-party compensation
≠ complete cross-organizational allocation

complete payment of the loss
≠ a durable remedy after insolvency or succession
```

This distinction prevents a technically successful or publicly generous customer response from erasing unresolved upstream responsibility and future remedy fragility.

## Legal and public-authority boundary

The fixture records synthetic contract, insurance, forum, and payment states. It does not apply real law or determine whether any clause is valid, enforceable, unconscionable, preempted, insured, or breached.

```text
uncompensated loss
≠ legal breach
≠ negligence
≠ misconduct
≠ manipulative intent

compensation
≠ consent to the original objective
≠ binding public authorization
```

PC-06 continues to govern public standing and objective control. PC-15 governs technical recovery across organizations. PC-16 governs allocation, payment, and remedy after the technical system is already recovered.

## Real-case promotion boundary

A real liability or remedy claim requires:

```text
legal entity, role, jurisdiction, and corporate relationship
complete contract, amendment, order form, and incorporated policy set
applicable law, forum, arbitration, and enforcement state
incident identity and complete loss evidence
causation theory, evidentiary burden, and adjudicated or agreed facts
notice, tender, reservation, denial, acceptance, and appeal records
indemnity, cap, exclusion, disclaimer, contribution, and subrogation terms
insurance policy, limit, deductible, exclusion, and payment history
direct affected-party standing and collective-claim route
settlement, judgment, fund award, or other enforceable disposition
payment receipts by payer and recipient
affected-party monetary and nonmonetary restoration
uncompensated and externalized loss
insolvency, substitution, succession, and remedy durability
public challenge, appeal, reopen, and authority state
```

## Custody chain

Each world emits a SHA-256 hash-linked chain:

```text
incident, organization, recovery, and loss snapshot
→ contract, indemnity, cap, contribution, and direct-right state
→ insurance coverage, reservation, and payment state
→ standing, causation, forum, appeal, and enforcement state
→ payer and affected-party payment ledger
→ uncompensated, externalized, delay, and succession state
→ liability-remedy mechanism classification
→ interpretation seal
```

Changing a contract term, policy state, standing right, forum, payment, residual loss, succession state, or classification breaks the chain.

## Publication boundary

The admissible laboratory statement is that complete technical recovery and one public `recovered` status can coexist with complete paid remedy, customer-only indemnity, capped liability, circular indemnity, upstream disclaimer, insurance exclusion, fragmented enforcement, or a causation-barred public fund. A real liability or remedy claim requires complete entity, contract, law, loss, causation, insurance, adjudication, payment, residual, enforcement, succession, and authority custody.
