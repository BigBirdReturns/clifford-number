# Preference custody laboratory floor v5

The preference-custody laboratory joins seven deterministic controls into one minimum identification, agenda, and authority floor for issue [#594](https://github.com/BigBirdReturns/clifford-number/issues/594).

The controls remain synthetic. They create no graph effect, thesis evidence, real-world causal conclusion, preference-change finding, discrimination finding, legitimacy verdict, agenda-suppression finding, manipulation claim, or inference of intent. Their purpose is to establish what a real deployment must preserve before observed behavior can be narrated as population preference, population support, a complete public agenda, distributional acceptability, or public authorization.

## The seven-control floor

### PC-01: exposure-policy confounding

A fixed 60/40 population produces an apparent preference above 93/7 when the institution exposes the two options at 90/10. Feeding the raw result back into the next exposure policy pushes the apparent majority above 95 percent although no preference changes. Logged exposure propensities recover the frozen population under the fixture assumptions.

```text
not exposed ≠ rejected
no click ≠ negative preference
raw engagement share ≠ population preference
```

### PC-02: option-set starvation

A fixed population prefers A, B, and C, while the institution offers only A and B. C-preferrers may fall back to one offered option or abstain. The same population therefore produces different observed distributions depending on the unavailable-option mechanism.

```text
unoffered option ≠ rejected option
choice among offered options ≠ latent first choice
fallback selection ≠ first choice
nonresponse ≠ negative preference
```

### PC-03: observational equivalence

Three materially different latent preference worlds produce the same aggregate 60/40 observed choice split. A model can therefore match behavior perfectly while remaining wrong about first choices, unavailable demand, and the mechanism that produced the behavior.

```text
same behavior ≠ same preference
behavioral fit ≠ identified mechanism
aggregate choice ≠ unavailable-option demand
predictive agreement ≠ public authorization
```

### PC-04: attrition and refusal censoring

Genuine preference conversion, selective exit, and strategic silence each produce the same normalized 80/20 observed headline. The full population outcomes differ in preference state, observed denominator, exit, and nonresponse.

```text
retained sample ≠ original population
exit ≠ preference conversion
nonresponse ≠ indifference
organized refusal ≠ missing data
normalized active share ≠ population support
```

### PC-05: subgroup response capacity and burden

Three materially different subgroup worlds produce the same aggregate 80 percent success rate. The balanced world has equal 80 percent group success and equal burden. The two asymmetric worlds place complete low-cost success in one group while the other group receives 60 percent success, a 40-point outcome gap, and fifteen times the adaptation cost per successful response.

```text
aggregate success ≠ subgroup parity
adaptation ≠ preference
compliance ≠ low burden
average gain ≠ cancellation of concentrated harm
missing group slice ≠ no disparity
distributional acceptability requires external authority
```

### PC-06: standing and objective-control authority

Four authority worlds preserve the same aggregate 80 percent support headline. Synthetic prediction and direct advisory feedback can support commissioner approval while remaining nonbinding. A binding affected-public instrument can authorize implementation when its overall and group thresholds pass. The same aggregate support is rejected when a protected group threshold fails.

```text
prediction = evidence, not authority
advisory feedback ≠ binding participation
institutional approval ≠ public authorization
aggregate support does not override a distributional rule
binding public rejection blocks implementation
model accuracy does not confer jurisdiction
```

The binding instrument seals the eligible constituency, quorum, overall threshold, group threshold, challenge window, and rights to amend the objective, suspend implementation, veto the proposal, appeal, and obtain remedy. The authority resolution records institutional approval, public authorization, binding rejection, and implementation state as separate objects.

### PC-07: agenda formation and collective option generation

Four agenda worlds preserve the same forced binary result:

```text
A = 800
B = 200
```

The complete synthetic rankings instead contain 800 first choices for C, 100 for A, and 100 for NONE. Six hundred people separately reject the objective being optimized.

Under a fixed agenda, A is implemented. Under advisory consultation, an attributed cross-group coalition proposes C with 800 supporters, but the proposal has no binding consequence and A remains. Under a binding amendment rule, C is admitted to the ballot and wins with 800 first choices. Under a binding objective challenge, the 60 percent rejection blocks implementation entirely.

```text
forced choice ≠ complete agenda
unoffered option may be collectively generated
popular suggestion without a binding rule ≠ agenda authority
preference among options ≠ acceptance of the objective
preliminary winner ≠ final public disposition
synthetic prediction cannot exercise amendment rights
binding objective rejection blocks implementation
```

The agenda chain seals the original option set, public proposal or challenge, coalition support by group, governing amendment rule, final ballot or objective disposition, and implementation consequence.

## The identification, agenda, and authority ladder

A conforming real-world case must preserve seven distinct stages.

1. **Exposure policy.** Who was eligible, what each person could encounter, and whether assignment was logged or randomized.
2. **Option set.** Every offered, excluded, rejected, unavailable, and never-generated alternative.
3. **Agenda formation.** Who could originate an option, form a coalition, amend the option set, challenge the objective, and bind the final agenda.
4. **Response mechanism.** Direct first choice, fallback, adaptation, compliance, or competing candidate explanations.
5. **Attrition and refusal.** Complete disposition, denominator history, exit, churn, nonresponse, organized refusal, and the reasons those states were assigned.
6. **Subgroup distribution.** Group denominators, assignment, outcomes, adaptation cost, burden, counterfactuals, and affected-group contestation.
7. **Public authorization.** A binding affected-public instrument that defines the constituency and decision rule, preserves objective amendment, suspension, veto, challenge, appeal, and remedy, and changes the implementation state when the public approves or rejects.

Passing a predictive benchmark answers fit under the tested instrument. It does not establish exogeneity of later behavior, completeness of the option set, legitimacy or completeness of the agenda, identification of the response mechanism, absence of dissent, population support, subgroup parity, acceptable burden, or authorization of the intervention. Human feedback remains advisory unless the governing instrument permits it to alter the agenda or bind implementation.

## Open frontiers

The laboratory floor remains deliberately incomplete. It does not yet qualify dynamic preference change in an open environment, negotiated package formation and collective bargaining, intersectional and unobserved group custody, model and policy succession, federated or multilevel authority, successor legitimacy, or a real deployment chain from synthetic output through intervention and feedback reuse.

Those remain open frontiers rather than being inferred from the seven controls.

## Promotion boundary

A real case requires the model and version, source population and data window, complete question and option set, option-origin and amendment history, attributed institutional and public dispositions, exposure and eligibility, complete disposition and denominator history, group denominators and burden distribution, counterfactual design, post-intervention outcomes, feedback reuse, a defined affected constituency, a binding decision rule with quorum and thresholds, enforceable agenda and objective-control rights, and an implementation or blocking receipt showing that the disposition carried consequence.

The laboratory can refuse unsupported interpretation. It cannot grant publication authority to a real-world causal, agenda-suppression, or legitimacy claim.
