# Criterion temporal causality, feedback, and post-treatment-bias custody

PC-26 separates a published causal-effect surface from the assignment, temporal, observation, counterfactual, interference, feedback, and lineage transactions required to support it. The control holds one public `causally_validated` status, a reported 0.20 average effect, a 0.12 to 0.28 confidence interval, a p-value of 0.01, two replications, one hundred scored decisions, one hundred published outcome records, and one consequential score-use label constant while changing the actual identification design.

The fixture is synthetic. It creates no graph effect, thesis evidence, named-policy causal finding, score-use authorization, consent finding, discrimination finding, coercion finding, manipulation conclusion, real-world causal effect, legitimacy verdict, public-authorization finding, or inference of intent.

## Eight identification systems

The qualified world assigns every eligible person through a predeclared concealed randomization, preserves fifty treated and fifty control participants, records complete compliance and follow-up, uses blind independent outcome adjudication, preserves no post-treatment conditioning or decision feedback, exposes no control person to treatment, uses a concurrent comparator, observes repeated baseline measurements and the untreated trajectory, logs the complete experiment lineage, and reproduces the same estimand through two independent replications.

This is the fixture’s one complete causal-identification path.

### Post-treatment mediator used as the criterion

The assignment is randomized, but the analysis conditions on a mediator observed after treatment and after the score-informed decision. One hundred outcome records are downstream of the treatment and decision.

```text
post-treatment conditioned records 100
decision-feedback observations     100
reported effect                    0.20
independent reference effect       0.05
```

Randomization at assignment does not repair a post-treatment estimand.

### Collider-selected outcome population

The public denominator remains one hundred because forty missing outcomes are imputed. Only sixty people are actually followed and observed, and observation depends on appeal or completion.

A complete published denominator is not complete observed follow-up.

### Control spillover and network interference

The design randomizes clusters, but thirty nominal control participants receive treatment exposure through unmodeled cross-cluster edges.

```text

stable-unit assumption            false
```

A control assignment is not an unexposed control state when interference is present.

### Historical control with time, policy, and population drift

The treated cohort is current. The nominal control cohort comes from a year earlier under a different policy, population state, and delivery channel.


A historical cohort does not supply a concurrent counterfactual without an explicit transport and pretrend argument.

### High-risk selection and regression to the mean

Fifty people are selected from the extreme tail of one noisy baseline measurement. Improvement is measured without repeated baseline assessment or an observed untreated trajectory.


Improvement after extreme-state selection is not automatically treatment effect.

### Adaptive policy and selective-label feedback

## Temporal order and post-treatment bias

The ledger orders score, assignment, treatment, decision, implementation, criterion observation, and follow-up. It separately records post-treatment mediators, descendants, decision feedback, treatment leakage, and reverse-causality paths.

A criterion observed after treatment can be a valid outcome. It becomes an identification failure when the analysis conditions on a post-treatment state, when the score-informed decision changes the criterion, or when the outcome is then narrated as a pre-treatment validation object.

## Observation and selection

The outcome ledger distinguishes:

```text
eligible people
followed people
observed outcomes
missing outcomes
imputed outcomes
selected outcomes
published records
```

Appeal, completion, survival, service use, and label availability can each create a selected observed population. An imputed record remains distinct from an observed person-level outcome.

## Interference

The interference ledger preserves the causal unit, network or cluster, spillover, treatment contamination, exposure mapping, and stable-unit assumption.

```text
assigned control
≠ unexposed control

cluster randomization
≠ no cross-cluster spillover
```

A networked or institutional intervention requires an exposure map or an interference-aware estimand.

## Historical controls and regression to the mean

Historical controls require calendar, policy, population, geography, channel, pretrend, and concurrent-comparator custody. Extreme baseline selection requires repeated measurements, baseline-noise analysis, and an untreated trajectory.

These mechanisms can generate a before-and-after improvement while preserving zero causal effect.

## Adaptive policies and selective labels

Adaptive score use changes both treatment assignment and outcome observation. A complete packet preserves exploration probability, logged propensities, label delay, selective labels, policy feedback, and an off-policy estimator.

```text
policy agreement
≠ unbiased effect

observed labels
≠ population outcomes
```

The admissible laboratory statement is that one public twenty-point effect, a narrow interval, a low p-value, two replications, one hundred published outcome records, and one `causally_validated` status can coexist with complete identification, post-treatment bias, collider selection, interference, historical controls, regression to the mean, adaptive selective labels, or version pooling.
