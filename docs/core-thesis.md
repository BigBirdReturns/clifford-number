## Working thesis

Post-Cold War governments externalized strategic capacity, specialist knowledge, risk, and implementation into private and hybrid institutions. Strategic ambiguity became a conversion technology: actors could move among adviser, investor, vendor, public-mission partner, infrastructure provider, and rights holder while authorship, accountability, risk, and reward remained distributed.

After approximately 2011, some founder-controlled technology, defense, capital, and policy networks appear to have pursued this coordination vacuum deliberately. The strongest proposition to test is not simply that these actors were connected. It is that they sought to shape missions, procurement gates, data environments, technical baselines, and institutional operating layers, then retain the resulting authority, market position, and residual value.

The project therefore reconstructs:

> how interests become infrastructure, how infrastructure becomes necessity, and how necessity becomes power.

This proposition remains divisible. Structural opportunity, opportunistic exploitation, deliberate market shaping, institutional replacement, coercive or ideological governance, and personal extraction are separate claims with separate evidence requirements.

## Why the project is realigning

The estate and game-trail work established that another uncontrolled expansion of names and nodes would add less value than deeper, instrument-level reconstruction. The project already has strong topology, custody, denominator, qualification, and publication machinery. The missing analytical unit is the **conversion chain**.

A conversion chain follows:

```text
public problem
→ authored framing
→ bounded option set
→ formal public gate
→ legal, financial, technical, or property instrument
→ allocation of control and risk
→ dependency or compulsion
→ residual private value
→ public contestability and exit
```

The central questions are now:

1. Where does public strategy end and private opportunity begin?
2. Who authored the available choices?
3. Was competition meaningful before the option set narrowed?
4. Which document converted adjacency or doctrine into authority, exclusivity, control, dependency, or residual value?
5. Could the public inspect, contest, substitute, terminate, migrate, recover, or reverse the result?
6. Who bore the downside and who retained the upside?

## Historical phase model

The dates are approximate and the phases overlap.

### P1 · Lean hegemon, 1991–2001

The state sought to preserve global reach while reducing permanent public personnel, industrial capacity, and administrative scale. Downsizing, contracting, consolidation, consulting, privatization, and globalized supply chains moved capability outside the public organization chart.

### P2 · Expeditionary contractor state, 2001–2009

Prolonged overseas missions were sustained without restoring mass mobilization. Contingency contracting, private logistics and security, intelligence support, emergency procurement, and mission integration created operational dependence and dispersed responsibility.

### P3 · Innovation bridge, 2009–2016

Commercial software, cloud, data, robotics, cyber, and venture-backed talent moved faster than ordinary procurement and public hiring. Innovation units, accelerators, fellowships, commercial-solution solicitations, and other transactions became bridge institutions—and therefore selectors.

### P4 · Dual-use platform state, 2017–2021

Peer competition and commercial control of frontier technology drove software-defined defense, national-security venture capital, cloud and data platforms, rapid acquisition, and strategic technology portfolios. Platform dependence, proprietary interfaces, and role stacking became central risks.

### P5 · Reindustrializing market-maker state, 2022–present

Governments turned to industrial subsidies, security conditions, milestone payments, export controls, research consortia, place-based clusters, and allied supply-chain coordination. Public risk and private control increasingly coexist inside the same instrument.

## Seven conversion stages

### C1 · Agenda formation

Who defined the public problem, supplied the vocabulary, elevated the threat or opportunity, and excluded alternatives before a formal gate existed?

### C2 · Option-set formation

Which people, firms, architectures, vehicles, and financing models became eligible, and who selected or validated them before formal competition?

### C3 · Public conversion

Which policy, appointment, appropriation, solicitation, evaluation, prototype, approval, grant, lease, order, or authority instrument made the private proposition publicly operative?

### C4 · Control architecture

Who acquired control over data, ontology, interfaces, standards, intellectual property, accreditation, integration knowledge, governance rights, land, debt, or contract options?

### C5 · Dependency and compulsion

When did an optional arrangement become preferred, default, required, technically indispensable, legally mandatory, or practically irreversible?

### C6 · Residual value

After public money, authority, infrastructure, and risk were applied, who retained equity, recurring revenue, intellectual property, data position, land value, governance rights, contract options, or strategic market position?

### C7 · Reversibility and counterpower

Could the public inspect, challenge, substitute, terminate, fork, migrate, recover, or reverse the system? Were meaningful competitors, unsuccessful paths, auditors, courts, protests, and ordinary controls preserved?

## Strategic ambiguity

Strategic ambiguity is not merely secrecy. It is the ability to claim the public or private identity most advantageous at a particular gate.

```text
private company      when public accountability attaches
public mission       when authority, subsidy, or legitimacy is needed

policy adviser       while shaping the option set
market participant   when the resulting opportunity appears

experimental vendor  while ordinary scrutiny is avoided
critical infrastructure when displacement becomes threatening

neutral technology   when political responsibility is questioned
ideological project  when recruiting capital, personnel, or allies

commercial product   when claiming speed and innovation
national-security necessity when narrowing debate or competition
```

The project tests four resulting asymmetries:

- authority without full public accountability;
- profit without full private risk;
- policy influence without visible authorship;
- coercive reach without formal sovereign responsibility.

These are questions to test, not labels automatically assigned to an actor.

## Persistent archetypes

The same functions recur through every post-Cold War phase:

1. **Convener** — defines the room, vocabulary, participant set, and legitimacy boundary.
2. **Translator** — converts one institution’s problem into another institution’s actionable language.
3. **Selector or portfolio curator** — narrows which actors and technologies receive capital, validation, access, or eligibility.
4. **Public market-maker** — uses authority, procurement, subsidy, land, infrastructure, standards, or demand to make a market viable.
5. **Personnel router** — moves knowledge, trust, and access across public, private, capital, and advisory roles.
6. **Paper architect** — writes the contracts, rights schedules, standards, grants, leases, deeds, governance instruments, and default rules.
7. **Integrator or platform owner** — assembles the system and may control interfaces, data flows, accreditation, or switching costs.
8. **Residual-rights holder** — retains durable upside after public authority, money, infrastructure, or demand has been applied.
9. **Legitimizer** — supplies technical, scientific, professional, civic, or institutional credibility.
10. **Adjudicator** — confirms, rejects, narrows, stays, audits, reverses, or otherwise tests the arrangement.

The analytical risk is not the existence of any archetype. It is **role stacking**: the same small network occupying agenda, selection, funding, evaluation, implementation, rights-holding, and review positions across the same decision window.

## Falsification obligations

The project must actively seek records showing:

- independent requirement formation;
- broad and meaningful competition;
- government ownership or control of data, interfaces, source, accreditation, and operations;
- practical substitution and migration;
- effective recusals and role separation;
- symmetrical treatment of competitors;
- proportionate private risk;
- public recovery on failure;
- ordinary career, investment, and convening patterns;
- and no actor-specific link from doctrine to implementation.

A conversion thesis that cannot survive these tests should be narrowed, superseded, or rejected.
