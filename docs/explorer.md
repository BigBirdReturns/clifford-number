# The explorer

Status: current (2026-11-08, release line 2.1.0). This page explains what a reader sees on [`explorer.html`](../explorer.html) and how to read it. The rules of the evidence model are in [`methodology.md`](methodology.md); the vocabulary is in [`definitions.md`](definitions.md); what each release changed is in [`releases/`](releases/2.1.0.md).

The explorer is a static page over compiled artifacts. The aperture reads four: `build/surface-graph.json` (actors, organizations, bounded surfaces and their participation rows), `build/hop-graph.json` (the admitted hop bases and shortest routes), `build/receipt-graph.json` (the receipts) and `graph.json` (the older research-network context). `app.js` loads three more for the rest of the page: `build/scores.json` (the entity pages' numbers), `build/public-catalog.json` (the case, track, claim and receipt catalogue) and `build/public-data-boundary.json` (the data-boundary notice). Everything on the page is derived from those seven files in the browser. Nothing on the page is a fact that is not in them, and the page never writes anything back.

## The display rules, once

Every view below obeys the same rules. They are stated here and not repeated.

1. **Every mark is one ledger row.** A dot, bar or node stands for one participation row or one surface row, never for a score or a summary of rows. The one exception is the printed accumulation of rule 6: a network node's size, halo and colour may sum its own rows by that formula, and its inspector lists every term.
2. **A hop is a surface, never a line between two people.** Where two people share a bounded surface on a documented day, the display shows the surface (a column, a diamond, a step card). No view draws a person-to-person line, and shared context never becomes an arc between two people.
3. **A dense roster is one object.** The Dialog public directory (112 listed names) is drawn as one block or one roster container with its count, and no line joins any two of its names. In Timeline, a name documented on no other surface (110 of the 112) gets no separate mark. A name that is also documented elsewhere (2 today: Matt Clifford and Dan Driscoll) keeps its own lane, and its Dialog row is drawn as one mark on that lane and counted once; the block's card names them.
4. **Undated rows are never time-sliced.** A row with no documented date is listed, never placed on an axis, and never dimmed or counted as inside or outside a time window.
5. **Evidence class and receipts stay attached.** Every mark, column, step and row carries its evidence class (official, primary public, reported) and opens its receipts.
6. **A visual channel may carry an accumulation only when its formula is printed on the page and every term is a documented row.** Heat is Σ touches weight(class) × 0.8^years, weights policy 1 · appointment 1 · procurement 2 · capital 2. No hidden composite (degree, clustering, machine or factory score) drives any channel.

   *Owner note, 2026-10-23.* "Take the blinders off. The incentives do all the work and nobody is steering. The data has filled up and the gut instinct is undeniable. Show capital, conviction and attention flow, with real names, with receipts. I want my heat map back and the network to feel alive." (the owner, 23 October 2026). Reason for the amendment: the 22 October removal of heat (commits ec5335fa, 677550f7, 8edeb467) cited a specification that is not in the repository; this amendment states its reason in the owner's words. The heat that returns is not the 1.3 composite (degree, clustering, surface, repeat and evidence terms): degree is still not salience, and every term of the new heat is a dated ledger row whose arithmetic the inspector prints.
7. **Counts name their denominator.** "N of M rows in window", "112 listed names", "15 named lanes": a number on the page says what it is a count of.
8. **Prominence is not an allegation.** Co-presence is never coordination. The page shows documented shared context and nothing more; the reader draws the conclusion, in their own name.

## The five aperture modes

The workspace arrives on the Field: the whole ledger as one force-directed field. Its five tabs sit directly above the view. A URL with no `ap_` parameters opens Field wherever its WebGL renderer can run (the hosted page); the standalone file bundles no WebGL module, so it opens Network and keeps Field as a tab with its counts, callout, tiles and rows. Existing links to Network and Timeline still open their saved views. Find in field or Find in network focuses a node; the separate page search opens a full record. Search remains available on record pages; opening a record or the connection checker replaces the visualization, and Explore returns to it. Method, Research queue, Estates and Game trails are in More. Dataset build dates and release metadata are under "About this dataset and release".

### Field (arrival)

The whole ledger as one force-directed field on a dark stage, in monospace chrome that follows the site theme (dark or light) so the five modes read as one deck; the stage itself is always dark. It reads `build/surface-graph.json` (every node and link), `build/hop-graph.json` (the anchor actor and the hop counts) and `build/receipt-graph.json` (the receipt counts); `graph.json` plays no part in it.

- **Nodes** are every actor, organization and bounded surface in the release: one node per ledger record. **Links** are the participation rows: one link from a participant to its surface for every row, coloured by the row's evidence class. No link joins two people or two organizations (rule 2). A dense roster is one node with its count; its rows are its spokes to that node, so no line joins two of its names (rule 3). A row whose participant is not in the actor or organization list still gets a node, labelled by its id and marked unlisted, so no row is dropped.
- **Channels.** Node size, halo and colour are the printed heat of rule 6 at t = the end of record (the last documented day), computed by `heat` in `src/social-field.js` from the node's own rows: a surface carries heat and its class colour, a person carries residue and the blend of the classes it touched, an organization carries residue and stays grey. Every node is drawn as one sprite, its kind as the shape (disc, square, diamond) and its heat as the glow around it; a faint glow floor is the same for every node and encodes nothing. Links are hairlines in their evidence colour; the rows of the selected node brighten and all others dim. The formula line stays on the stage legend. Layout position is the force engine's output and carries no meaning: no channel reads it, and nothing on the page is a degree, clustering or layout statistic. (Rows into a dense roster, a surface of 40 or more participants, have a shorter rest length in the layout so the roster stays one object; that is layout, not a channel.)
- **The header strip** prints plain counts from the compiled artifacts, each with its denominator: nodes by kind; links (the participation rows, split actor and organization); receipts, and how many carry an archive record; admitted hops, and hop-eligible surfaces of all surfaces; the documented range with the undated count; and t.
- **Selecting or searching** a node (a click, a name label, Find in field, a row in the table, a step in a tile) highlights it and its neighbours, dims everything else without removing it, and docks the inspector beside the node as a callout: label, kind, the documented count, the heat or residue line with every term (one per dated row, undated rows named apart), its rows in order, its receipt ids (each opens the receipt) and actions (open the public record, find a route, open in Network, clear). On a phone the inspector is the bottom sheet. A thin ring follows the pointer over the node a click would take, and the selection ring keeps at least 14 px of radius at any zoom; Escape clears, as Clear does. The callout opens with a breadcrumb (Field › lens › kind › name; the Field and lens crumbs return to the whole field, lens kept) and arrives from the node's side (instant under reduced motion). On arrival the anchor actor of `build/hop-graph.json` (the actor every Clifford Number counts hops to) is selected; Clear returns to the whole field. `ap_field_sel` carries the selection in exact-view links.
- **Instrument tiles** under the field, one ledger fact each with its denominator: rows by evidence class (of all rows); surfaces by heat class (of all surfaces, the class read from `surface_type`); hop edges (admitted actor pairs, hop-eligible and refused surfaces of all surfaces, pairs refused for no overlap); receipts by archive kind (of all receipts); rows per documented year 2016 to 2026 as bars (of all rows, with the counts before, after and undated named; undated rows are never placed, rule 4); and the selected node's trajectory (its dated rows in order, undated rows listed after). No tile shows a composite, a percentage without its denominator, or a layout statistic.
- **Lenses.** All, Decisions, Hunt, In force. A lens keeps the nodes and rows that satisfy one recorded field and dims everything else without removing it; it sums, ranks and weighs nothing, and the heat channels are untouched. *Decisions* keeps the surfaces whose recorded `status` begins `official_` (an award, contract, appointment, filing, register or payment record) and the rows that name them; its tile lists the statuses as recorded with counts. *Hunt* keeps the surfaces recorded `hop_eligible: false`, draws them hollow and colours them by their recorded `hop_refusal_reason` (grouped into families for colour only; the tile lists every reason as recorded) with the rows that name them: the refused self-reports pending review, the organization-only instruments with no named person, the reported-only rows awaiting an upgrade. *In force* keeps the rows with no end date or an end date on or after t and the nodes they join; its tile splits them by heat class. The legend prints the active lens's definition and its counts with denominators; Records in this view lists the lens's rows when nothing is selected; `ap_field_lens` carries the lens in exact-view links (`all` at the default).
- **Records in this view** lists every participation row of the selected node, or of the active lens, or of the whole field, oldest first and undated rows last, 50 to a page.
- **Export** produces a field packet: the counts, the selected node's heat terms and every row of the current scope.
- The field pauses when the stage is off-screen, the tab is hidden or another mode is active. Ink never magnifies: a node's pixel size is fixed at its size in the fitted view, so zoom changes spacing, never glyphs; wheel and pinch zoom toward the pointer, the +/− buttons toward the centre. Nearer than 55% of the fitted distance, a row whose two ends are both off-screen is faded out and a chip on the stage names how many rows that is, of all rows, until the camera brings an end back (edge locality). The layout runs once per visit and the arrival fits the body of the field (the span between the 1st and 99th percentile of node positions, so a few far specks do not shrink the rest); under a reduced-motion preference the layout settles before the first paint and camera moves are instant.
- Where WebGL is unavailable, or in the standalone file, the Field tab keeps its counts, callout, tiles and rows and says that the live field is not drawn.

### Timeline

A ledger timeline: rows on lanes against a date axis.

- **Lanes** group the rows by People (default), Organizations or Surface types. By default each view shows the 18 entities with the most dated rows (ties by label; a dense-roster row does not count, so a name listed only on a roster never gets a lane); everyone else sits in one strip labelled "Other people (N)", "Other organizations (N)" or "Other surface types (N)". In the default view a lane does not grow: a cluster shows two marks and folds the rest into "+N", so 18 lanes fit the stage. Up to 18 named lanes can be shown; "Add a lane" searches people, organizations and surface types. (Owner decision 2026-10-23, replacing "every actor on two or more surfaces", which grew from 15 to 117 lanes with the intake promotion.)
- **A mark** is one participation row at its documented date. Fill is evidence class; outline says whether the surface is hop-eligible. A row with an end date is a span; a year or month row is widened to the period it names; an open-ended row runs to the edge of the axis; rows within a few pixels fold into one "+N" object that still counts each row.
- **A column** is one hop basis: a surface that two or more people share on one day, drawn as segments that touch only the lanes of that surface's participants. The 95 hop bases in the release form 19 such surface-day objects on 18 days.
- **The roster block** is the Dialog directory, drawn once with its count (rule 3). Its card lists the names also documented elsewhere and says how many appear on no other surface.
- **Controls:** Date range offers Recent activity (the responsive default), individual years and Full record (the entire documented range). Filters & tools contains Add a lane, the Surfaces filter (all, hop-eligible only, context only); the evidence floor; "Mark a window" to dim rows outside a [from, to] window without removing them; Reset. Ctrl (or Cmd) + wheel zooms the axis; a plain wheel scrolls the page. The overview strip under the plot carries the axis handles.
- **Keyboard:** Tab reaches one mark, then the arrow keys, Home and End walk the days and lanes; the lane labels are a second stop.
- **Cards:** every mark, lane, column, block and fold has an inspector card with its role, window, evidence class, receipt buttons and actions (open the record, add lanes, route from here, open the roster view). The first card, "Select a mark", offers a reading guide and an example. On phones it stays below the chart until a selection opens the detail sheet.
- **URL keys:** `ap_time_by`, `ap_time_from`, `ap_time_to`, `ap_time_cursor`, `ap_time_lanes`, `ap_time_sel`, `ap_time_evidence`, `ap_time_hop`, written only when they differ from their defaults.

### Network

The Network tab is the research network as a spherical web on a dark canvas, with neutral surrounding controls. Named labels identify a small set of existing nodes without overlapping; selecting a name or a node focuses its neighborhood and opens its source records. It uses WebGL when available and SVG scenes otherwise. Initialization is shared across concurrent requests so a second empty renderer cannot replace the populated canvas.

- **Nodes** are actors, organizations and surfaces. Their channels are the printed heat of rule 6, computed in `src/social-field.js` (`heat`, `applyFieldHeat`) from the ledger rows of `build/surface-graph.json`:
  - **Class.** Each surface has one class read from its `surface_type`: *policy* (policy document, government advisory, campaign, model governance, category formation), *appointment* (board advisory, directory roster), *procurement* (procurement contract, customer vendor, defence industrial), *capital* (employment investment, founder officer, public-private fund, laundering-chain connector). An unknown type is policy. Weights: policy 1, appointment 1, procurement 2, capital 2.
  - **Heat of a surface at t** = Σ over its dated participation rows with date ≤ t of weight(class) × 0.8^years, where years = (t − the row's documented start) / 365.25 (a year or month row starts on the first day of the period it names). Undated rows contribute nothing and are listed as such; rows dated after t are not yet counted. Surface core size and halo come from heat (logarithmic, so the 113-row Dialog roster stays one block on one node); surface colour is its class.
  - **Residue of a person or organization at t** = the same sum over its own dated rows, kept per class. A person's colour is the four class colours blended by their share of its residue; an organization stays grey. Size comes from residue. A node with no ledger row (research context only) keeps its ontology fill and has no halo.
  - **t** is the end of the time window; with no window it is the end of record (the last documented day). Moving the window or pressing Play time recomputes heat and residue in place.
  - **Colours:** policy #3987e5, appointment #c98500, procurement #d95926, capital #199e70. The stage legend and the Options panel print the key and the formula line "heat = Σ touches weight(class) × 0.8^years; policy 1 · appointment 1 · procurement 2 · capital 2".
  - **The inspector** prints the denominator for the selected node, for example "heat 2.15 = 2 touches at 2026-01-01: capital 2 × 0.8^0.26 = 1.89 (2025-09-27) + policy 1 × 0.8^6.00 = 0.26 (2020-01-01)", followed by the per-class split and the undated rows. The tooltip names the documented count and the heat or residue.
- **Arcs** are the sourced edges of `graph.json`, one for one, coloured by evidence class; the canonical overlay arcs run from a participant to a surface. Their rule is unchanged by heat. Dated activity pulses keep their rule too: a pulse is drawn only inside its row's window. Every node is one sprite in a single draw and every arc one slot in a single line draw (the per-node pick proxies the library needs stay invisible), so the sphere renders in a handful of draw calls; a click on an arc selects the arc nearest the pointer within 9 px.
- **Controls:** Find in network, zoom and Reset view are always visible. **Options** contains **Depth**, a slider whose stops are Whole network, Neighborhood, Bounded surface and Evidence; **Evidence time**, a [from, to] window with two handles and an "All time" button. Out-of-window arcs and pulses dim and are never removed; the header reads "N of M rows in window". Play advances the window over 52 seconds; it is off by default and never starts under a reduced-motion preference.
- **Selecting an arc** opens what it is: a participation row (surface, role, window, evidence class, receipts, "Open public record") for an overlay arc, or the claim with its receipts for a research arc.
- **The inspector** lists every ledger row of the selected object, oldest first. A row the field cannot draw is still listed and says why: "not drawn on the field (privacy guard)" or "drawn as one contained roster object, not as an arc".
- The field pauses when the stage is off-screen or the tab is hidden. Options includes selectors for a surface family and named surface.

### 03 Route

The shortest admitted route between two actors as actor → bounded surface → actor steps, from `build/hop-graph.json`.

- **Controls:** From actor, To actor (and a swap button), "Active during" (a year, month or day), the evidence floor, Reset.
- Each step is a surface card with the two roles, the validity window, the evidence class and the receipt count; selecting a step opens its exact roles, window, receipts and archive state. A route longer than 24 steps is shown in a window of 24 with the whole path retained and reachable.
- When no route survives the controls, the view says so and, where the compiler recorded a refusal (two participations on one surface whose dates do not overlap), shows it as a blocked step with both windows. "No route" means not documented in this corpus; it is never proof of absence.

### 04 Roster

One bounded surface as a roster container, for surfaces with two or more actors.

- **Controls:** the surface; "Find actor or role"; "Active during"; the evidence floor; "People shown" (6 to 36 actors shown at once, 18 by default); Clear pins.
- Participants appear in an aligned grid within one source container. Selecting a person opens their recorded role, dates, evidence class and receipts. A count identifies the additional people available through search or a higher display limit. No participant-to-participant line is drawn, and pinned actors stay visible.

### Shared by every mode

- **The evidence overview** under the stage is a stable, paged table of the rows the view is showing (25, 50 or 100 per page). Selecting a row selects the object; sorting and paging never reorder the row under the pointer.
- **Copy exact view** puts the current view, as `ap_` parameters, into a link. **Saved views** keep several such views in the browser and compare them. **Export** produces a publication packet (a printable page with the receipt union and the interpretation caveat) for the current view.
- **The evidence dialog** opens any receipt or claim. The aperture publishes the mode and object a receipt was opened from (`globalThis.__cliffordApertureOrigin`), but `app.js` does not read it yet, so at this release the dialog opens with its plain toolbar label. A breadcrumb naming the mode and object is part of the entity-page contract below.
- Hash routes (`#actor/...`, `#surface/...`, `#desk/...`) keep the aperture's `ap_` state, so a shared link restores the view the sender had.

## The connection checker

The main navigation’s "Check a connection" opens a dedicated view. It takes two actors and an optional date and answers from `build/hop-graph.json`:

- the Clifford route (minimum hops) first, always, and labelled canonical;
- optionally a secondary projection (strongest evidence, best dated, official only), clearly labelled as secondary and shown as an explicit absence when none exists;
- for each step, both roles, the overlap window and its precision, the evidence class and the receipts;
- the connections the compiler refused and why;
- copy-ready caveat language for publication.

## The entity pages

Searching, or following any name, opens a record page under a hash route: `#actor/<id>`, `#organization/<id>`, `#surface/<id>`, `#chain/<id>`, and from 2.1.0 also `#type/<surface_type>` and `#year/<yyyy>`. The pages are fed from the same derived model as the Timeline, so a row on a page and a mark on the timeline are the same ledger row. Every page shows:

- fact chips with their denominators ("10 documented surfaces of 54", "across 6 surface types", "with 8 organizations", first and last dated day);
- a date-sorted ledger table (row number, date or window with precision, surface, role, organizations, evidence class, receipts) with a status line that is never silent: it says how many rows are shown of how many, and what any filter hid;
- "Patterns in the rows": surface-type counts, organization counts, hop partners with the day of their shared surface (each opens the connection checker with both names), then counted co-presence with dense surfaces excluded and labelled;
- a single-lane mini timeline above the ledger table and an "Open in the timeline" button that opens the Timeline tab on that person, organization or surface;
- "Copy ledger as text"; a machine-derived profile, where no hand-written one exists, labelled as such.

A surface page adds the surface's window with precision, every participant with their own window and role, the evidence-class mix, hop eligibility and the ledger's refusal reason; the Dialog surface page lists its roster sorted by "Also documented on". Nothing on a page is a score: the "Structural context index" and "Factory Score" of 1.3.0 are gone.

## What the page is not

The explorer is a reading instrument over published records. It does not compute Clifford Numbers (the compiler does, in `tools/`), it does not fetch anything beyond the compiled artifacts, and it does not add, weigh or rank evidence beyond the one printed heat formula of rule 6, whose every term is a listed ledger row. A number of hops is a count of documented shared surfaces on a route and nothing else.
