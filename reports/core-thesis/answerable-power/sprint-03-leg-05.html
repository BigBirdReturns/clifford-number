Governing question

Exit fault lines

IDNameObservationRequired control
EXIT-F01formal ownership versus operating capacityA public body may own data or contract rights while lacking the people, interfaces, security, models, documentation, and authority required to operate independently.Test every essential capability and named operational responsibility after vendor withdrawal.
EXIT-F02product exit versus enterprise exitA bounded dashboard, workflow, or service may migrate while the institution remains dependent on a larger platform or common layer.Map the entire dependency tree and state the exact exited function.
EXIT-F03new vendor versus new dependencySubstitution can move custody and control to another private layer without increasing public sovereignty.Compare rights, interfaces, custody, portability, staffing, and exit conditions before and after.
EXIT-F04transition completion versus residual custodyOperational cutover does not prove deletion, license termination, support cessation, or removal of historical copies and derived outputs.Require residual-custody inventory, deletion or retention authority, and independent verification.
EXIT-F05public capacity versus democratic governanceA public institution may regain technical control while affected people remain outside purpose, category, threshold, and use decisions.Join R5 to an explicit R6 constitution rather than treating public ownership as self-legitimating.
EXIT-F06reported savings versus full value ledgerLower running cost can omit migration expense, hidden cloud or supplier dependencies, error cost, staff burden, and distribution of recovered capacity.Reconcile total cost, performance, public IP, institutional learning, and affected-party value.
EXIT-F07plurality versus substitutabilityMultiple vendors or prototypes can coexist while one common data, identity, interface, accreditation, or hosting layer remains indispensable.Operate a component-removal or alternative-baseline counterfactual and measure continuity.
EXIT-F08successful cutover versus durable sovereigntyA transition can succeed once but fail after staff turnover, emergency pressure, administration change, platform updates, or successor procurement.Test the replacement over time, under incident load, and through one further modification or vendor change.
